**Thinking traces against the no-think baseline at matched compute on textbook72 and hard712 dev.** The layout and estimator are those of `fig_tts_uplift`, here for the round-2 pairs on textbook72 (72 problems; seed 0 in a, seed 1 in b; k = 64 for both arms) and the seed-0 round-2 and round-3 pairs on hard712 dev (500 problems, in distribution for these models; thinking k = 32, baseline k = 64). Top: pass@k against sampled tokens per problem (prompt prefill excluded; linear interpolation in log k), with the thinking-trace model at a 64-token think budget per step (solid) and its no-think baseline trained on the same proofs with the same EI rounds (dashed). Each curve starts at one attempt (marker: pass@1) and ends at its last sampled attempt. Bottom: thinking − baseline in percentage points, drawn only where both reads cover the token count. In (a) the thinking model was also read with thinking off (budget 0, dotted). The grey stroke marks its best budget at each compute level; that is budget 0 only below the cost of one 64-budget attempt (488 tokens), where the grey bottom-row segment is the best-budget difference. Bands are 95% bootstrap intervals over problems (4,000 draws shared by all arms; one training seed per arm, so seed variance is not included). The y-axes start at 0. On textbook72 thinking is ahead at matched tokens in both seeds: +3.5 [+0.4, +7.5] points at 8k tokens for seed 0, and +5.4 to +6.2 from 1k to 8k for seed 1. The set is small, though, and 58 of its 72 problems informed hard712's design. On hard712 dev thinking is behind at 1k tokens (−3.0 and −2.5) and tied from 2k. With thinking off, the seed-0 model is 14.9 [8.5, 21.5] points below the baseline per attempt, so for this model a 64-token budget beats no thinking wherever it can run.
