# Does supervised mid-training on long glued proofs make RL generalise to longer proofs? (ab3/midtrain)

A monitor-run experiment approved by the PI. One seed. Results are in `report/report.md`, generated by `analyse.py`
and summarised below. Run 2026-09-26 04:25-07:34 UTC.

## Conclusion

**No. Mid-training on long glued proofs does not make RL generalise to longer proofs.** After the same RL (one
seed), the long arm is worse than the base arm on the natural long theorems. No RL arm solves any standard theorem
past length 13, and at most 0.2% of the matched constructed theorems at 14.

1. **Natural theorems at L12-14** (never glued; the main evidence).
   - long + RL solves 2.9%, against 5.3% for base (-2.4 points [-3.4, -1.5]), 4.4% for short (-1.5 [-2.4, -0.6])
     and 4.1% for more (-1.2 [-2.2, -0.3]).
   - At L13, long reaches 0.01%, against 1.2% for base.
2. **Chain theorems at L15-18** (a glue type never seen in training): 0% for every checkpoint, before and after RL.
   So are the ANDI theorems at 15-18 and the glued 22-26 set after RL.
3. **In distribution** (L6-11): every mid-trained arm is slightly better than base after RL (long +3.0 points [+1.8,
   +4.4]). Held-out greedy on cap-6 is 0.98 for all four RL arms.
4. **Mid-training does teach something, and RL removes it.**
   - Before RL, the mid-trained long model is the only checkpoint that solves anything at length 15 or more: 1.4%
     of the ANDI theorems at 15-18 and 0.15% of glued 22-26.
   - Before RL, 27-31% of its attempts at 15-18 are at least L_true long (median 12-15 lines), against 0-2% for the
     other pre-RL checkpoints. Its failures on the long set are ordinary invalid steps: premise errors are only 6% of
     failures.
   - After the arm-C RL, which trains only on proofs of at most 11 written lines, those solves are gone. Attempts at
     15-18 are back to a median of 10-11 lines, with at most 1% long enough.
   - On the long set, 49% of failures are again premise-copying errors (73% for base), and the failure hazard at
     line 5 rises from 0.02 to 0.52.
5. **Weight of the glued evidence.** The glued 22-26 test is built exactly like sft_long (3-part ANDI), so even the
   pre-RL solves there are close to in-distribution. They are weak evidence for length generalisation.

**Reading.** In this setup, RL capped at 11-line proofs overwrites whatever long-proof ability supervised
mid-training added, including the ability to copy long premise blocks. Whether RL without the line cap, or with long
targets, would keep it was not tested.

All numbers are in `report/report.md` and `report/results.json`. The caveats (test_long is close to in-distribution for the long arm, the 3-part share is tied to length, prompts are long, test_long labels are upper bounds, one seed) are listed there.

## Question

- **The earlier finding.** In lengthexp, RL on theorems of true length 6-11 raises pass@1 up to length 11 but not
  past it. Attempts stop at 6-10 written lines whatever the theorem needs.
- **The question.** Does it help to show the model long proofs first, by supervised fine-tuning on long glued proofs
  before the same RL?
- **The controls.**
  - *short:* the same amount of glued proofs, but short ones. It separates "long" from "glued".
  - *more:* the same number of fine-tuning steps on cap-6 records only. It separates "long" from "more training".

## Design

| arm | pretrained start | mid-training | RL |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | 039 s0 | none | arm-C recipe |
| long | 039 s0 | 2,000 glued ANDI proofs of 16-24 lines + 8,000 cap-6 records, 3 epochs | arm-C recipe |
| short | 039 s0 | 2,000 glued ANDI proofs of 6-11 lines + the same 8,000 cap-6 records, 3 epochs | arm-C recipe |
| more | 039 s0 | 10,000 cap-6 records (the same 8,000 + 2,000 more), 3 epochs | arm-C recipe |

- **Start.** 039 s0 is the released pretrained checkpoint
  `/scratch/cowifornia/nd-rl/checkpoints/runs/039_muon-adamw-finish-5pct_s0/pretrain.pt` (sha256 `08e2644a…`),
  which is `ab3/frozen_039/pretrain.pt`.
- **Mid-training** (`midtrain.py`): the seed recipe's own fine-tune path, `rl_C.finetune`.
  - Whole records are packed into rows of at most 1,024 tokens and never truncated.
  - The line-index shift has room for 24 lines, the longest proof in any mix.
  - AdamW, lr 3e-4 with 35 warmup steps and cosine to 3e-5, batch 128.
  - 234 steps, which is 3 epochs of 10,000 records. Steps, batch, schedule and seed are identical in all three arms.
  - Not metered, as for pretraining. Steps, tokens, wall time, loss and held-out greedy before and after are logged
    in `ckpt/mt_<arm>.json`.
- **Checkpoints and frozen copies.** The mid-trained checkpoint is written in the harness's `pretrain.pt` layout: the
  same keys as the 039 file, with `state` replaced and `stage = midtrain_<arm>`. `make_frozen.py` builds
  `frozen/<arm>/` as a copy of `frozen_039` with only `pretrain.pt` replaced, verified by sha256 in
  `frozen/<arm>.check.json`.
- **RL.** All four arms use the arm-C recipe: `rl_C.py` is a byte copy of `lengthexp/rl_C.py`, with `max_len_train`
  = `max_proof_lines_train` = 11. The RL pool is run 2's targets, the budget is `DEFAULT_FLOPS` 3.9e15, and the seed
  is 101, run with `--no-score`. base runs through `../frozen_039/harness.py`, the others through
  `frozen/<arm>/harness.py`.
- **Evaluation** (`mt_evaluate.py`: lengthexp's `evaluate.py`, unchanged, pointed at `testsets/` and `eval/`).
  - k = 32 at T = 0.8, max_new 1,024.
  - Checkpoints: 039 s0 (`pre039`), each mid-trained checkpoint before RL (`mt_long`, `mt_short`, `mt_more`) and
    each arm after RL (`base`, `long`, `short`, `more`).
  - Test sets:
    - `testsets/std.jsonl`, `matched.jsonl`: byte copies of lengthexp's final draw.
      - Natural theorems at 6-14; constructed ANDI and chain at 15-18.
      - The chain family at 15-18 is reported separately, as a glue type absent from mid-training.
    - `testsets/long.jsonl`: `build_long.py` from `data/test_long.jsonl`, 150 held-out glued 3-part ANDI theorems at
      each of 22, 24 and 26 lines, longer than any training proof.
    - Disjointness: 0 pkey overlaps with every training and test file, and 0 parts shared with the SFT data
      (`testsets/long_MANIFEST.json`).
- **Data** (`data/`, built by the data agent; `data/POOLS_MIDTRAIN.md`, `data/SHA256SUMS`): `sft_long`, `sft_short`
  and `test_long`. Every SFT proof was re-checked with nd_verify before training (0 failures).

## Files

| file | what it does |
|---|---|
| `midtrain.py` | mid-training for one arm, and `--smoke` for a code-path test |
| `make_frozen.py` | builds a frozen harness copy for a mid-trained arm and checks it |
| `rl_C.py` | the arm-C RL recipe (byte copy of `lengthexp/rl_C.py`) |
| `build_long.py` | builds `testsets/long.jsonl` and its disjointness manifest |
| `mt_evaluate.py` | evaluation wrapper |
| `pipeline.py` | runs everything after the data arrived, through the GPU lock |
| `analyse.py` | writes `report/report.md`, `report/results.json` and the figures |

**Outputs.**
- `ckpt/`: mid-trained checkpoints and logs.
- `runs/<arm>_s101/`: harness outputs. `final.pt` is the RL checkpoint.
- `eval/<label>/`: per-theorem rows, unit summaries and samples.
- `logs/`: pipeline, mid-training and frozen-copy logs.

## Notes on the run

- **Another user's jobs.** GPU jobs of another user ran on GPUs 1-4 outside our lock during the run. A shared card
  only slows a job, because the RL budget is metered in FLOPs. But a much slower RL run could reach the harness's
  3,600 s wall cap and stop early. So `pipeline.py` starts each RL run only on a GPU that is lock-free and has no
  other user's process, pinned to that GPU. Evaluations are unaffected by sharing.
- **Missed long units.** The first pipeline start evaluated with evaluate.py's default lengths (6-18), which skip the
  long set. The restarted pipeline passes `--lengths 6-18,22,24,26` and fills in only the missing units.
- **Stopped queue ticket.** One RL harness process (arm 'more') was stopped while it was still waiting in the lock
  queue, before it had written anything. Restricted to two busy GPUs, it would have blocked the evaluations queued
  behind it. It was relaunched later by the pipeline.
