# Does the proof representation change length generalisation? (ab3/repr)

A monitor-approved experiment (PI design). No hill-climbing agent reads anything here. Work log: `STATUS.md`.
Results: `report/report.md` (generated by `analyse.py`), summarised in "Results" below.

## Question

The 4-layer d=256 prover (3.2M parameters) trained by RL on theorems of true length 6-11 (lengthexp arm C) solves
12.7% at L11 (pass@1), 3.4% at L12, 0.2% at L13 and nothing at 14-18. Its attempts are too short: the median
attempt has 6-10 written lines at every test length, and from L14 on at most 0.3% of attempts reach L_true. Most
failures are ordinary invalid steps around line 4-5. Only 4-12% of failed samples at most lengths have a first bad
line that states the conclusion (lengthexp/report/fake_finish.md; corrected by the monitor on 2026-09-25). On long
tests the attempts that survive die near the arm's own training maximum (lengthexp/report/fig_hazard.png).

Does the way a proof is **represented** help it generalise past the training length, when training stays on
L_true 6-11? Only the representation changes: the data, the recipe and the compute are the same for every variant.

## Design

Five variants x three seeds. Every variant uses the same parameter budget (3,210,240 parameters, identical to the
baseline: no variant adds a parameter), the same 99-token vocabulary, and works with the KV cache.

| variant | tokenizer | positions seen by attention |
|---|---|---|
| `abs` (baseline) | abs: text verbatim, line tokens N1..N64, random index shift in training | RoPE on token positions |
| `rel` | rel: line numbers dropped, a citation of line j on line i is B<i-j> | RoPE on token positions |
| `posrand` | abs | RoPE; in training each sequence's positions are a sorted random subset of range(1024) (Ruoss et al. 2023) |
| `nope` | abs | none (causal mask only; Kazemnejad et al. 2023) |
| `posline` | abs | two-part rotary: 8 of the 16 rotary pairs per head turn with the line index, 8 with the offset in the line |

Pipeline for each variant x seed (approved by the PI):

1. **Pretrain** with the pretraining team's run 039 recipe (`039_muon-adamw-finish-5pct`: Muon on the block
   matrices for 95% of training, then AdamW on everything) for a **fixed 2,911 steps** (039_s0's step count). The
   039 runs used a 300 s wall clock on an A40, and our cards are 150 W 3090s, so every variant gets equal optimizer
   steps instead. Data: `frozen/data/train_cap6.jsonl`, which is byte-identical to 039's `data/train.jsonl`
   (sha256 9c024cd9...). Seeds 0, 1, 2 (the released 039 seeds). `pretrain.py`.
2. **RL**: the run-3 seed recipe configured as length-experiment arm C (`max_len_train = max_proof_lines_train =
   11`, copied from `lengthexp/rl_C.py` into `rl_repr.py`), at the harness's `DEFAULT_FLOPS` = 3.9e15. It runs
   through a frozen-copy harness of the variant (`frozen/<variant>_s<seed>/`, built by `make_frozen.py`) with
   `--no-score`. RL seed 101 + pretraining seed (arm C's seeds), so the three seeds draw arm C's three target subsets.
3. **Evaluation** with lengthexp's evaluator (`evaluate.py`: a copy with the variant loader). k = 32 samples per
   theorem at T = 0.8, max_new 1024, on the standard test sets L6-18 and the matched constructed set L6-14.
   The sets are lengthexp's final draw (built 2026-09-25 02:27 UTC), frozen as a copy in `testsets/` so that every
   variant is scored on the same theorems (sha256 std 62db9018..., matched 172e376e...).
4. **Analysis** (`analyse.py`, a sibling of `lengthexp/analyse.py` that imports its statistics). Per variant:
   pass@1 and pass@4 per length; log-odds drop per extra line past 11 with seed intervals; share of attempts at
   least L_true long; first-error position; the per-position failure hazard on the long sets (P(fail at line p |
   lines 1..p-1 valid), the most direct sign of a length-specific circuit: a spike near line 11-13 means the model
   dies where its training data ends); in-distribution checks (held-out greedy on cap-6, pass@1 at 6-11); and
   for rel, citation coverage and the split described below.

## Choices and why

**Fixed steps, and the 039 reproduction check.** `pretrain.py` is 039's `pretrain.py` line for line, except that
the schedule fraction is (steps done) / 2,911 instead of elapsed / 300 s: the cosine, the AdamW switch at 95%,
warmup, batch 256, lr, Muon settings, val split and data order are all 039's. Before trusting the variants, the abs
re-pretrain is compared with the three released 039 checkpoints, both measured with the same code on our cards
(report section 1). Measuring the released checkpoints with our code reproduces 039's own reported numbers
exactly: held-out greedy 98.8%, 98.85%, 98.75%; val loss 0.0933, 0.0967, 0.0935.

**pos-rand: sorted random subset, not a random offset.** RoPE is relative: rotating every query and key by the
same extra angle leaves every attention score unchanged. So a random offset per sequence is a no-op, up to
rounding (`tests/test_variants.py::test_rope_offset_invariance_is_why_posrand_uses_subsets`), and would just be a
second baseline. A sorted random subset of range(1024) changes the relative distances that attention sees. Each
training sequence of n tokens gets `sorted(sample(range(1024), n))`, the same in pretraining and in RL fine-tuning.
The draws come from a generator of their own, so the data order and index shifts equal abs's. 1024 is the
brief's range and covers every real test sequence (at most ~800 tokens at L18).
Ruoss et al. use the same random procedure at test time (Sec. 3). The brief says to sample and evaluate at offset
0. **This needs the monitor's decision** (see STATUS.md and "Open questions"). At 300 smoke steps the pos-rand
model scores 0% held-out greedy on positions 0, 1, 2, ... and 25% on training-style random positions. After the
full 2,911 steps it still scores 0%, against 74-77% on random positions (Results). The
random-position evaluation (`evaluate.py --pos-eval rand`) draws, per sample, a sorted random subset of
range(1024) of size len(prompt) + 19 x L_true, the length of a typical correct proof (19 = arm C's mean tokens per
written line). Tokens beyond that continue at the subset's mean gap.

**pos-line: two-part rotary rather than position coupling.** Line structure is what a proof is made of: citations
point k lines back, boxes open and close on lines, and every line is a short formula with strict token order.
Position coupling (Cho et al. 2024) gives all tokens of a line one position, which throws away the within-line
order the formula syntax needs (lines are up to ~50 tokens, with nested brackets). The two-part rotary keeps both:
position = (line, offset). The line is the number of line breaks (';' and the prompt's closing 'PRF') before the
token: prompt = line 0, proof line i = line i, QED opens the line after the last one. The offset is the token's
place inside its line (or prompt). Of each head's 16 rotary pairs, 8 turn with the line and 8 with the offset,
each half using RoPE's geometric spectrum (base 10000) over its own pairs. It adds no parameters. Positions are a
function of the tokens so far, so the KV-cache samplers carry (line, offset) along (`GPT.next_pos`).
Consequence: past L11 the model still extrapolates in lines (up to 18 vs 11 in training). But the offset stays in
the trained range: test prompts have at most 163 tokens, RL-pool prompts up to 205, and lines rarely pass ~50
tokens. Line counts are no longer confounded with token counts.

**rel: no augmentation.** rel has no line-index tokens, so abs's index shift (and the seed recipe's capped shift,
`index_room`) does not apply. In rl_repr.py the cap is applied to the abs tokenizer only; for rel it would have
renumbered the B<k> ids as if they were line indices. Citation coverage is uneven (monitor's note, 2026-09-25):
pretraining proofs cite at most 5 lines back, so B6+ never occur in pretraining. Report section 8 gives the counts
per k for pretraining and for each rel run's RL-trained proofs, and splits rel's long-length results by whether
the theorem's reference proof needs a citation beyond the trained range. Per the monitor, no augmentation was
added for this.

**nope** is the baseline with the rotation removed (`pe='none'`). Nothing else changes.

## What changed relative to the approved code (all representation plumbing)

- `lib/` = `frozen/lib` with `model.py` (the positional variants, stored in the checkpoint cfg; a key is stored
  only when it is not the default, so abs and rel checkpoints have exactly the frozen cfg), `sample.py`
  (positions from the model: `init_pos` / `next_pos`), and a new `posenc.py` (training positions). For pe='rope' the
  code computes exactly the frozen values: bit-identical logits, tested.
- `rl_repr.py` = `lengthexp/rl_C.py` with its CONFIG and SMOKE unchanged (tested). Edits: the samplers take
  positions from the model (the CUDA-graph step carries (line, offset) for pos-line and skips the rotation for
  nope), fine-tuning batches take per-record positions from `posenc`, and the index cap applies to abs only.
  For abs it behaves exactly as rl_C.py. Harness smoke runs of both on the released 039 s0 checkpoint give
  identical found proofs, sampling statistics, losses and compute (`tests/compare_smoke.py`).
- `frozen/<variant>_s<seed>/harness.py` = `ab3/frozen/harness.py` with three textual edits (`make_frozen.py`):
  the tokenizer mode and model variant are read from `pretrain.pt` instead of the hard-coded abs / plain GPT. The
  meter, metrics, gpulock, scorer and `DEFAULT_FLOPS` are unchanged. The diff is in each copy's `REPR_MANIFEST.json`.
- `evaluate.py` / `sampler.py` = lengthexp's with the variant loader, model-driven positions and `--pos-eval rand`.
  For abs they sample exactly what lengthexp's sampler samples (tested on arm C's C_s101 checkpoint and on 039 s0).
- The harness's own scorer (`score.py` -> host daemon) is not used (`--no-score`, as in lengthexp). The daemon's
  frozen sampler knows only RoPE models, so it could score abs and rel but not pos-line or nope.

```
$PY tests/with_gpu.py -- bash tests/run_smoke.sh       # smoke pipeline for all variants
$PY pretrain.py --variant abs --seed 0                 # one pretrain (lock: --gpu auto) -> pretrain/abs_s0/
$PY pretrain.py --ref 0                                # released 039 s0, same metrics -> pretrain/ref039_s0/
$PY make_frozen.py --label abs_s0                      # frozen/abs_s0/ from pretrain/abs_s0/pretrain.pt
$PY frozen/abs_s0/harness.py --rl rl_repr.py --out runs/abs_s0 --seed 101 --gpu auto --no-score
$PY evaluate.py --ckpt runs/abs_s0/final.pt --label abs_s0
$PY evaluate.py --ckpt runs/posrand_s0/final.pt --label posrand_s0_randpos --sets std --pos-eval rand
nohup $PY launch.py run --hold-rl posrand > logs/run.out 2>&1 &     # the whole pipeline, restartable
$PY launch.py status
uv run --script analyse.py                              # -> report/report.md, report/*.png, report/results.json
```

`launch.py run` keeps at most 4 jobs holding or waiting for a GPU (FIFO lock, so other approved jobs interleave).
Each job is at most ~35 min (pretrain ~13 min, RL ~30 min, evaluation ~14 min). It starts seed 0 of every variant
first, skips finished stages, and does not retry failures.

## Results

Four of the five variants ran the full approved pipeline: abs, rel, nope and pos-line, each with 3 pretraining
seeds, 3 RL runs and 3 evaluations, and no failures. pos-rand is pretrained (3 seeds). Its RL and evaluation are
on hold, awaiting the monitor's decision (see "Open questions"). Numbers are seed means with the [min, max] over
seeds. Full tables: `report/report.md`.

**Conclusion: no representation change improved length generalisation.** Past the training length every variant
falls off the same cliff. pass@1 is 0.0-0.6% at L13 and 0.0% at L14-18 for every variant (at most 0.01%; pass@4
at most 0.1%). On the long tests the few attempts that survive die at lines 12-13, right past the training
maximum, whatever the positional encoding.

| variant | pass@1 L6-11 pooled | L11 | L12 | L13 | L12-18 pooled | log-odds slope per line, L11-18 [95% t] | held-out greedy after RL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| abs (baseline) | 47.7% | 23.2% | 9.6% [8.6, 11.2] | 0.6% | 1.57% | -1.53 [-1.94, -1.12] | 98.6% |
| nope | 47.2% | 25.0% | 9.6% [6.5, 12.9] | 0.4% | 1.53% | -1.66 [-2.34, -0.97] | 98.8% |
| pos-line | 46.3% | 22.3% | 4.2% [3.1, 4.9] | 0.0% | 0.65% | -2.16 [-2.63, -1.68] | 98.5% |
| rel | 28.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.00% | not estimable | 98.7% |

- **nope equals abs.** Removing the positional encoding entirely changes nothing measurable at any length:
  L12 9.61% vs 9.64%, pooled L12-18 1.53% vs 1.57%, slope -1.66 vs -1.53 (Welch p 0.54). RoPE positions beyond
  the trained range are therefore not what limits length generalisation here.
- **pos-line is a little worse just past the training length.** L12 4.2% vs 9.6% (Welch p 0.008; exact
  permutation p 0.10, the smallest possible with 3 vs 3). The slope over L11-18 is steeper, -2.16 vs -1.53
  (Welch p 0.013). In distribution there is no difference (L6-11 46.3% vs 47.7%, p 0.21). Its failure hazard spikes
  at line 12, the first line index it never trained on (0.71 vs 0.51 for abs, pooled over L12-18). On L15-18 its
  attempts are longer (5-10% reach L_true lines vs 0.1-0.3% for abs), but they still die at lines 12-13 (hazard
  0.83 / 0.99). So an explicit line coordinate extrapolates no better than token positions, and slightly worse.
- **rel is capped by its vocabulary, not by relative citations as such.** Pretraining proofs cite at most 5 lines
  back, so B6..B64 are never trained. The rel model never samples them: 0.00% of the citations in its L12-18
  samples, and none of its RL-trained proofs has a citation beyond B5 (tables below). abs's RL-trained proofs
  have thousands of citations at distances 6-9. So rel cannot write any proof that needs a jump longer than 5
  lines. The L12-18 test theorems have 1,835 reference proofs, and 1,834 of them need such a jump. rel already collapses in distribution: L9 8.3% vs
  36.1%, L10 0.9% vs 28.2%, L11 0.0% vs 23.2%. During RL it solved 0-3% of its L_true 10-11 targets (abs: 39-63%).
  Its first errors are ordinary rule errors (85%), not bad B-tokens: when it needs a long jump it cites a nearer,
  wrong line. This is the untrained-token handicap the monitor predicted, stronger than expected. The experiment
  therefore does not test relative citations fairly (see "Open questions").
- **Failure position.** The hazard P(fail at line p | lines 1..p-1 valid) on L15-18 rises to 0.81-0.97 at
  p = 12-13 for abs, 0.94 at p = 12 for nope and 0.83-0.99 at p = 12-13 for pos-line. rel's reaches 1.00 at
  p = 11. First errors on L6-14 sit at line ~5 for every variant. Attempts stay short: the median attempt has 6-11
  written lines at every test length, and from L14 on at most 0.3% of attempts reach L_true (pos-line: 1-10%).
- **In distribution nobody is broken** except rel from L8 up. Held-out greedy after RL is 98.5-98.8% for all four.
  The 039 pretrain lifts the whole curve relative to lengthexp's arm C (older pretrain, same RL): abs L11 23.2% vs
  12.7%, L12 9.6% vs 3.4%. It does not move the cliff: both are ~0 from L13-14.
- **Matched construction control** (report section 10) shows the same ordering: abs ≈ nope > pos-line past L11 >> rel.

In shares: pretraining citations are 53% B1, 31% B2, 11% B3, 2.8% B4, 1.6% B5 and 0% beyond. The split the monitor

In finer bins of the reference proof's longest jump (6 / 7-8 / 9-10 / 11+), abs solves 0% / 8.8% / 2.2% / 0% and
rel 0% everywhere. No variant solves any theorem whose reference proof needs a jump of 11 or more.

**pos-rand (not run past pretraining).** All three pos-rand pretrains score 0.0% held-out greedy on contiguous
positions 0, 1, 2, ... (val loss 0.59-0.67 vs 0.094). On training-style random positions they score 74-77% (val
loss 0.093, the same as abs). An evenly spaced grid at the training density gives 78.7% (seed 0,
`tests/posrand_diag.py`, `pretrain/posrand_s0/posrand_diag.json`). A model trained on random subsets of
range(1024) has not learned to decode on contiguous positions, and it decodes ~20 points worse than abs even on
training-like positions.

**Compute used:** 11.1 GPU-h (pretrain 2.8, RL 5.8, evaluation 2.4; `report/record/derived.json`, which also
states the method), plus ~0.4 GPU-h for tests, smokes and the pos-rand diagnostic. The estimate was ~15-16 GPU-h; the difference is pos-rand's held RL and evaluation.

## Open questions for the monitor

1. **pos-rand sampling and evaluation positions** (STATUS.md, 02:39 UTC). The options:
   (a) sample and evaluate on random positions (Ruoss's own test-time procedure), with contiguous as the check.
   This needs a position-table path in rl_repr's CUDA-graph sampler, and pos-rand would start RL ~20 points
   behind in distribution.
   (b) contiguous as instructed: RL starts from 0% and cannot find proofs, so the variant fails for a trivial
   reason.
   (c) change the training scheme (e.g. mix contiguous sequences in): a design change and re-pretrain.
   (d) drop pos-rand.
2. **rel needs B6+ coverage to be a fair test.** With the current data it can never cite more than 5 lines back.
   Any fix is an augmentation, e.g. a pretraining or RL batch transform that spaces citations out, or a
   curriculum. Per your instruction none was added; this is a design decision.

## Caveats

- **Per-token losses are not comparable across tokenizers.** rel drops one easy token per line. Held-out greedy
  and pass@k are the comparable measures.
- **rel proofs are shorter in tokens**, one token per line fewer. Under the same FLOP budget, rel therefore gets
  slightly more samples per unit of compute. That is part of the representation, not a confound to remove.
- **Three seeds give little power.** The exact permutation test of 3 vs 3 runs cannot go below p = 0.10. The
  t-interval uses t = 4.30.
- **Reference proofs define "needed citation distance"** in the rel split: one proof per theorem (minlen proof,
  else constructed proof). Another valid proof might need shorter jumps.
