## 2. My independent account of each paper's measures

### 2603.22446 "Sparse but Critical"

- **Text.**
  - Sequences generated by the RL policy, scored teacher-forced by both base and RL (Sec. 2, opening).
  - AIME24/25, 32 samples per problem (Sec. 2.1); vLLM with temperature 1 and top-p 0.7 (A.2.1); top-p 1 on the fine-tuning data.
- **Scorers.** Base and RL model, on the same prefixes.
- **Quantity.**
  - Per-position Jensen-Shannon divergence between the base and RL next-token distributions (Sec. 2.1).
  - It is computed on the **top-p-truncated** distributions. Entropy and probabilities come from the full distributions.
  - A robustness check with top-p = 1 is in A.5 (Figs. 30, 32).
- **Positions.**
  - All response positions (Figs. 2, 3).
  - A high bin JS > 0.1 and a low bin JS < 0.1 (Fig. 4); JS < 0.01 for the word clouds (Fig. 5).
  - The rank and tail analyses use JS > 0.1 positions only (Figs. 9, 22, 10).
- **Claims.**
  - "Near-zero" divergence at more than 83 % (DAPO) and more than 98 % (SimpleRL) of positions (Sec. 2.2).
    - In Fig. 2 these are the first histogram bin: 0.8380 and 0.9858.
    - The bin edge is not given. **[inference]** It is about 0.0125 judged from the axis.
    - The DAPO percentiles in Fig. 2b are: 90th 0.0756, 95th 0.1544, 99th 0.3431.
  - Divergence is higher at the start and the end of responses (Sec. 2.3, Fig. 3, Fig. 26b "Near the answer span"). The paper interprets the late rise as "answer formatting and termination behavior".
  - At JS > 0.1 positions, top-k overlap is high for k >= 2 (Fig. 9).
  - About 30 % of RL top-1 tokens are already base top-1, and more than 80 % (DAPO) / 90 % (SimpleRL) lie within base top-3 (Fig. 22).
  - About 5 % of RL top-1 tokens have base probability < 0.01 under DAPO, near 0 under SimpleRL (Sec. 4.2, Fig. 10a).
  - Training evolution (Sec. 4.3, Fig. 11):
    - Qwen2.5-Math-7B DAPO intermediate checkpoints, scored on the final checkpoint's (200) outputs.
    - **The JS is against the base.** Every percentile is 0 at step 0 and rises monotonically, so this is distance from the start, not distance to the final model.
    - The Jaccard of the JS > 0.1 set with the final set rises late (Fig. 11b).
  - Cross-sampling: 1.53-10.1 % of effective swapped tokens recover or erase the RL gains (Table 1, Table 4).
  - SFT gives denser shifts (Sec. 2.6).
- **Error bars.**
  - None on any token statistic or on the cross-sampling tables (those are Mean@32).
  - The only spread is Table 2 "± over 3 runs", undefined.

### 2605.06241 Akgül et al.

- **Text.** The **base model's greedy (T = 0)** response on MATH-500 (Sec. 3, 3.1).
- **Scorer.** The RL "teacher" teacher-forces the base text.
- **Quantity.** Eq. 3, by argmax:
  - UNSHIFTED: the argmaxes are equal;
  - RERANKED: the teacher's argmax differs from the base's but is in the base top-5;
  - SHIFTED: the teacher's argmax is outside the base top-5.
- **Table 2 (four pairs).**
  - Reranked 2.09 / 1.03 / 3.96 / 2.29 %.
  - Shifted 0.02 / 0.01 / 0.12 / 0.02 %.
  - Entropy ratio (base entropy at reranked positions over unchanged ones) 7.58-12.63x.
  - Mean base rank of the teacher's token, among reranked positions, 2.14-2.39. It is 1-indexed: "second most likely".
- **Internal inconsistencies.**
  - The Sec. 3.1 text says "zero shifted positions in any pair", and the abstract says the promoted token "always lies within the base model's top-5"; Table 2 says otherwise.
  - The reranked share is given as 1-3 % (abstract), 1.0-4.1 % (Sec. 3.1) and 1-4 % (Fig. 1).
  - The entropy ratio is given as 5-12x (text), 7-12x (Fig. 1) and 7.58-12.63x (table).
  - Sec. 2.1 says the mechanistic analysis covers GRPO, PPO and RLOO; Table 2 has no RLOO pair.
- **Oracle (Sec. 3.2).** It substitutes the teacher's argmax at every disagreement during greedy base decoding.
  - **[inference]** This reproduces the teacher's greedy decode token for token, so "reproduces the teacher's pass@1 exactly" holds by construction.
  - The informative parts are the random top-20 substitution control and the entropy gate (Sec. 3.3: τ = 1.2, 1.2-8.3 % of tokens).
- **KL-LoRA (Sec. 4, App. B).** Rank 32, QKVO, 100 problems, teacher rollouts at T 0.6 / top-p 0.95, top-64 logits.
- **Error bars.** None anywhere. Most Table 3 cells are single-generation pass@1; AIME and AMC are avg@8.

### 2506.01939 Wang et al.

- **Sec. 3 (entropy patterns).**
  - Text: Qwen3-8B in thinking mode, on its own T = 1.0 samples on AIME24/25, more than 10^6 tokens.
  - Quantity: the entropy of the generating model, at the decoding temperature (Eq. 1).
  - Result: 50.64 % of tokens have entropy < 1e-2, and the 80th percentile is 0.672.
- **Sec. 4, Table 1 (entropy-pattern overlap).**

  - Fig. 4: entropy change is larger at high-entropy positions.
- **Sec. 5 (training on high-entropy tokens).** Policy gradient on the top 20 % entropy tokens per batch matches or beats all tokens on 8B/14B/32B; training on the bottom 80 % is worse (Table 2, Acc@16).
- **Error bars.** None.
- **Not in the paper.** No rank, NLL or loss-concentration statistic.

The note is careful and substantively correct. I checked every quoted number against the text and they match. The list below is everything I would change.

**Errors and overstatements**

3. **"All three papers study maths RL on base or non-thinking models".**
   - Akgül's Qwen3-4B teacher is General-Reasoner: GRPO with a model verifier on multi-domain data (their Table 1).
   - Wang Sec. 3 analyses Qwen3-8B, a post-trained thinking model (its RL runs start from base models).
   - Paper 1 also evaluates on GPQA.
   - Minor, but the sentence should be "mostly maths RL from base models".
4. **Typo.** "Math maths" in row 1, item (iv).
5. **Row 4, Paper 1 Fig. 11 design.** The note calls the design comparable to ours. That is fair for the text (the final model's outputs scored by intermediate checkpoints). But it omits that **Fig. 11a measures JS against the base** (0 at step 0, rising), not against the final model. Our `tokens_summary.json` JS and excess measure distance *to* s2300, and the footprint's NLL is distance to s2300's tokens. So the trajectories run in opposite senses; only Fig. 11b, the Jaccard with the final divergent set, points towards the final model.

**Omissions**

8. **Paper 1's answer-span result.**
   - What the paper reports: Paper 1 does analyse the region near the answer (Fig. 26b, local JS by distance from the answer span). It interprets the late rise as answer formatting and termination (Sec. 2.3).

## 4. Are the step-0 data scored by the unadapted checkpoint? Yes.

- **`wrap_lora` (olmo_edl.py:200).** It calls `get_peft_model` with the default LoRA init: A kaiming-uniform, B = 0, dropout 0. With B = 0 every LoRA layer adds exact zeros.
  - `reset_lora` (230) re-inits the adapter and asserts `not lora_B.any()`.
- **Order of operations.** `cmd_run` → `setup` (load, then `wrap_lora`) → `load_pool` → `run_edl`.
  - `run_edl` (998) calls `compute_step0` **before** any `run_one`, which is where training happens.
  - `compute_step0` scores under `torch.inference_mode`, `model.eval()`, and writes `step0_tokens.npz` through `save_token_stats`.
  - When `step0.json` already exists with a matching key, it is reused. A restarted process re-wraps, so it is again inert.
- **Provenance.**
  - Each `step0.json` `utc` equals the start time of that directory's first EDL row: 11:10:35Z for dpo, 11:20-11:25Z for the others.
  - The npz mtimes precede every adapter directory.
- **Reset check.** Every completed row at all five checkpoints records `reset_check.abs_diff = 0.0` nats. That is the first online score after `reset_lora` compared with the step-0 value for the same example. So the step-0 numbers are the inert-adapter numbers.
- **Consistency with step0.json.** `tokens_summary.json` records that the npz per-response sums equal `step0.json` to at most 1.8e-12.
- **Same text across checkpoints.** I checked that all five npz files have identical pid, set, think_end, offset and token arrays. I also checked that the test-set mean NLL from each npz equals the L0 in `step0.json` (0.62048, 0.61166, 0.58343, 0.54296, 0.50203).
- **Minor notes (no correction needed for the section).**
  - The `identity` block in `step0.json` does not fingerprint weights. s0100-s2300 share the same config and index hashes, so the checkpoint identity rests on the pod paths. The checkpoints do differ: their NLLs differ.
  - The train set is scored at batch 1, while test and reach are scored right-padded in batches. This can cause bf16-level differences between sets, which are negligible here.

Table 2; Sparse but Critical Sec. 2.2) come from other models, domains, texts and decoding settings and are not comparable numbers (literature/footprint_papers.md). **C7 (Interpretation, bullet 2).**

Replace:

It is not a divergence and not a count of positions that RL changed, and it cannot be read as "RL changes few tokens". The s2300 row has no misfit at all, yet it is the most concentrated (worst 10 % carry 58.0 %): a temperature-1 sampler pays most of its surprisal at a few uncertain positions. As a checkpoint approaches s2300, its NLL profile approaches this self-surprisal profile, so rising concentration is expected even if the misfit does not move. Where the misfit itself sits is measured by the concentration of the excess NLL over s2300 (tokens_summary.json): the top 5 % of positions by excess carry 69 % of the net excess at dpo and 110 % at s1100. Shares above 100 % arise because many positions have negative excess.

**C8 (Interpretation, bullet 3).**

with:

> Whether the answer-segment pattern reflects formatting and termination is not tested. The answer segment's high concentration is also partly mechanical: when most positions have near-zero NLL, the few remaining positions carry nearly all of a small total.
