## 1. "Sparse but Critical" (Qwen Pilot Team, arXiv 2603.22446, ICLR 2026)

**What is measured.**
- Divergence: per-position JS between base and RL next-token distributions, Section 2.1: `JS(x<t) = D_JS(pi_base(.|x<t) || pi_RL(.|x<t))`, bounded in [0, log 2]. "divergences are computed on top-p truncated distributions using the same sampling configuration employed during generation, while entropy and probabilities are from the full estimated distributions" (Section 2.1).
- Text scored: "we take sequences generated by the RL policy and evaluate both models' conditional distributions at each token position" (Section 2, opening). Sampling: "nucleus sampling with top-p = 0.7 and temperature = 1" on AIME 2024/25, 32 responses per problem (A.2.1, Section 2.1); top-p = 1 on fine-tuning data. So the text is the RL model's own temperature-1 (nucleus) samples, both models score it teacher-forced. This is the same direction as ours (final model's samples, earlier model scores).
- Sparsity (Section 2.2, Fig. 2): histogram and percentile curves of JS. "Under DAPO, more than 83% of token positions exhibit near-zero divergence, while this proportion exceeds 98% under SimpleRL." No numeric threshold for "near-zero" appears in the text. Per the auditor, who inspected Fig. 2 as an image, "near-zero" is the first histogram bin (0.8380 DAPO, 0.9858 SimpleRL; bin edge not stated, about 0.0125 by [inference] from the axis) and the DAPO 90th/95th percentiles are 0.0756 and 0.1544, so roughly 5-10 % of DAPO positions have JS > 0.1; that is not comparable with our 7.1 % (dpo vs s2300, top-20 truncated JS, T=1 text) because Paper 1 truncates to top-p 0.7; Fig. 4 and later use JS < 0.1 and > 0.1 as low and high bins, and Fig. 5 uses JS > 0.1 and < 0.01.
- Position (Section 2.3, Fig. 3): mean and median JS against normalised position. Entropy relation (Section 2.4, Fig. 4): entropy distributions in low (< 0.1) and high (> 0.1) JS bins.
- Rank and overlap (Section 4.1): top-k overlap between base and RL candidate sets at JS > 0.1 positions, "computed as the size of the intersection divided by k" (Fig. 9); "Around 30% of RL top-1 tokens are already ranked first under the base model, and over 80% (DAPO) and 90% (SimpleRL) fall within the base top-3" (Fig. 22, JS > 0.1 positions only). Tail: share of RL top-1 tokens with base probability < 0.01 is about 5 % (DAPO), near zero (SimpleRL) at JS > 0.1 positions (Section 4.2).
- Training evolution (Section 4.3, Fig. 11): "using intermediate checkpoints from DAPO training on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, we track token-level distributions while conditioning on the final model's (checkpoint 200) outputs". JS (against the base, 0 at step 0) percentiles rise monotonically; Jaccard of the high-divergence set with the final set rises.
- Cross-sampling (Section 3, Algorithm 1 in A.6): switch to the other policy where JS > epsilon; Table 1/Table 4 give the effective share of swapped tokens (3.86 % SimpleRL, 7.8 % DAPO forward on AIME24; 5 % and 10.1 % reverse), accuracy at 8k tokens.
- Divergence-weighted advantage (Section 5, Table 2): Overall average 42.48 +- 1.35 baseline, 44.92 +- 0.05, 45.05 +- 0.79, "averaged over 3 runs".

**Models.** Qwen2.5-32B base with SimpleRL (GRPO) and DAPO; Qwen2.5-Math-7B (two clip-higher settings), Qwen3-8B-Base, Mistral-Small-24B (A.4, A.5). Maths (AIME 2024/25), plus GPQA-Diamond (graduate-level science multiple choice, not maths) for part of the analysis (Figs. 28-29); SFT comparison on Qwen2.5-32B (A.4), where SFT's divergence is "substantially larger" (Fig. 12).

**Error bars.** None on the divergence histograms, percentile curves, rank figures or Table 1/4 (single numbers; AIME has 30 problems). Only the Section 5 training table has a spread (mean of 3 runs, +- as printed, undefined in the text I read). The bootstrap used in our footprint has no counterpart here.

**Not found in the paper.** No loss (NLL) concentration, no share-of-nats statistic, no thinking/answer split (these are not thinking models except as the reasoning style of base models; Qwen2.5 base outputs have no separate answer segment).

## 2. Akgul, Kannan, Neiswanger, Prasanna (arXiv 2605.06241v3)

**What is measured (Section 3.1, Eq. 3).** "we generate a response from the base model and, at every token position, we record which token the RL-tuned teacher model would have preferred given the identical prefix." With deterministic decoding ("MATH-500 with deterministic decoding (T=0)", Section 3):
- UNSHIFTED: `argmax pi_base = argmax pi_teacher`.
- RERANKED: `argmax pi_teacher != argmax pi_base` and `argmax pi_teacher in Top-5(pi_base)`.
- SHIFTED: `argmax pi_teacher not in Top-5(pi_base)`.
- Table 2 (four pairs: Qwen2.5-1.5B, 7B GRPO; 7B PPO; Qwen3-4B GRPO): reranked 2.09 / 1.03 / 3.96 / 2.29 %, shifted 0.02 / 0.01 / 0.12 / 0.02 %, entropy ratio 7.58 / 8.27 / 9.41 / 12.63 x, mean rank of the teacher's token 2.30 / 2.14 / 2.39 / 2.25. "entropy ratio" is the base-model entropy at reranked positions over that at unchanged positions (the text says "5-12x", Fig. 1 says "7-12x", Table 2 shows 7.58-12.63).
- Oracle intervention (Section 3.2, Fig. 2): at reranked positions replace the base token with the teacher's argmax; reproduces teacher pass@1; random top-20 substitution does not. Entropy gate tau = 1.2 (Section 3.3).
- KL-LoRA distillation (Section 4, Eq. 4; Appendix B): rank-32 QKVO adapter on 100 problems, teacher rollouts at temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, top-64 logits cached.
- ReasonMaxxer (Section 5-6, Table 3).

**Text scored.** The base model's own greedy responses on MATH-500, scored by the teacher (the opposite direction to ours and to Paper 1: base text, RL model scores). The statistic uses argmaxes, not sampled tokens.

**Error bars.** None for Table 2 or Figures 1-2 as extracted (single numbers, one decoding run, MATH-500 has 500 problems). Table 3 has no intervals either; AIME/AMC are avg@8, other sets single-sample pass@1.

**Internal inconsistencies (checked against the text; they confirm the earlier note).** Section 3.1 says "we observe zero shifted positions in any pair" while Table 2 lists 0.01-0.12 %. The abstract says "Only 1-3% of token positions are affected" while Section 3.1 says 1.0-4.1 %. Section 6.2 says ReasonMaxxer is "on par with or better than the RL baseline" in every case while Table 3 has e.g. Qwen3-4B MATH-500 0.660 (ReasonMaxxer) against 0.746 (General-Reasoner) and Qwen2.5-7B average 0.472 against 0.485 (Open-Reasoner-Zero). The Qwen2.5-1.5B base model scores 0.233 MATH-500 pass@1 in Appendix Table 4 and 0.298 in Table 3: base accuracies differ between tables (different prompt or sampling, not explained in what I read).

**Oracle caveat.** The oracle substitutes the teacher's argmax at every disagreement during greedy base decoding, which reproduces the teacher's greedy decode token for token, so "reproduces the teacher's pass@1 exactly" holds by construction and is not causal evidence. The informative parts are the random top-20 control and the entropy gate (Section 3.3, tau = 1.2, 1.2-8.3 % of tokens). Further inconsistencies: Section 2.1 says the mechanistic analysis covers GRPO, PPO and RLOO but Table 2 has no RLOO pair; the abstract says the promoted token "always lies within the base model's top-5" while Table 2 lists 0.01-0.12 % shifted.

**Not found.** No JS, no NLL concentration, no per-checkpoint training trajectory, no thinking/answer split (Appendix C: Qwen models are prompted with a raw completion prompt, no chat template).

## 3. Wang et al., "Beyond the 80/20 Rule" (arXiv 2506.01939v2, NeurIPS 2025)

**What is measured.**
- Token entropy (Eq. 1): `H_t = - sum_j p_tj log p_tj`, `p_t = softmax(z_t / T)`. "In off-policy settings ... The entropy is still calculated using pi_theta" (Section 2.1): entropy is that of the scoring model, on whichever text.
- Section 3: Qwen3-8B (a thinking, post-trained model), AIME'24/'25, T = 1.0, thinking mode forced, "collect over 10^6 response tokens". Findings: "the entropy of over half the tokens (approximately 50.64%) is below 10^-2, while only 20% of tokens have entropy greater than 0.672." Threshold h = 0.672 is the 80th percentile of that sample. Temperature-split decoding (Eq. 5, Fig. 3).
- Section 4, Table 1: DAPO on Qwen3-14B base; "we generate 16 responses per question across the six benchmarks" with the reasoning model after RLVR, "compute logits using reasoning models from various RLVR stages and identify those in the top 20% entropy", then the overlap of high-entropy positions with the base and the final model: base overlap falls from 100 % to 86.67 % at step 1360, RLVR-model overlap rises from 86.67 % to 100 %. Fig. 4: average entropy change per 5 % entropy percentile of the base. This is the same design as ours (final model's samples, earlier checkpoints score them), but the statistic is overlap of top-20 % entropy position sets, not rank or NLL.
- Section 5, Table 2 and Figs. 6-8: DAPO with policy gradients only on the top-20 % entropy tokens per batch (Eq. 6) against all tokens, Qwen3-8B/14B/32B base; Acc@16 and Len@16.

**Error bars.** None. Tables report single Acc@16 values (e.g. Qwen3-32B AIME'24 55.83 against 63.54); Figures 6-7 are single training curves. Small differences on the 8B model (+0.53 average) are not accompanied by intervals.

**Not found.** No rank, no NLL, no JS, no loss-concentration, no segment split. The paper itself says its Llama result is "less convincing" (Section 5.4).

## Our read versus theirs

Our footprint (`footprint.py`): the final model's (s2300's) own temperature-1 responses (134 pool responses, thinking plus answer, cap 6,144 tokens), scored teacher-forced by each untouched checkpoint; per position the stored token's rank (rank 0 = checkpoint's top-1) and NLL under the checkpoint.

| Our number | Closest quantity in the papers | Where it differs | Comparison valid? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top-1 share (rank == 0) of s2300's *sampled* token under checkpoint t | Akgul: share of positions with `argmax_teacher == argmax_base` (1 - reranked - shifted, e.g. 96-99 %). Paper 1: "Around 30% of RL top-1 tokens are already ranked first under the base model" (JS > 0.1 positions only) | (i) Ours uses the sampled token, theirs the teacher's argmax. Our s2300 row (83.4 % total top-1 for s2300 scoring itself) is a reference level, not a cap: another model could rank s2300's sampled tokens first more often. Theirs is argmax against argmax and has no sampling floor. (ii) Text: ours is the final model's temperature-1 samples, Akgul's is the base's greedy text, Paper 1's is the RL model's top-p 0.7 samples. (iii) Paper 1's 30 % is conditional on JS > 0.1, ours is over all positions. (iv) Maths vs instruction-following thinking model. | No. The only like-for-like quantity would be argmax agreement, which `tokens_summary.json` has (88.1 % at dpo) but which this footprint does not recompute. |
| Top-5 share (rank < 5) | Akgul's RERANKED + UNSHIFTED complement: `1 - SHIFTED` (99.88-99.99 %); Paper 1: "over 80% (DAPO) and 90% (SimpleRL) fall within the base top-3" at JS > 0.1 | Same as above (sampled token vs argmax; text differs). Our top-5 for s2300 itself (98.4 %) is again a reference level, not a ceiling. The matching statistic to Akgul's SHIFTED is `s2300's argmax in checkpoint's top-5` (99.56 % at dpo in the earlier tokens note, so 0.44 % shifted), which is about 4-40x their 0.01-0.12 %. | No for the share; the argmax-based analogue exists in the earlier note and can be compared in kind (different model, domain, text, greedy vs T=1 samples), not as a test of the same hypothesis. |
| Share of nats carried by the worst 1 / 5 / 10 % of positions (ranked by the checkpoint's own NLL) | None. Paper 1's percentile curves are of JS between two models. Wang's top-20 % is a split by entropy, not by loss; their Table 1 overlap and Fig. 4 are about which positions are high-entropy. Our earlier tokens note used excess NLL (checkpoint minus s2300) ranking, which is closer to a divergence ranking but is a different statistic | The paper quantities rank positions by divergence or entropy; ours ranks by the checkpoint's own surprisal on tokens it did not generate. High NLL positions are those where the final model sampled a token the checkpoint finds unlikely; this overlaps with but is not identical to high divergence. | No direct comparison exists. At most a qualitative statement that, as in all three papers, a minority of positions carries a large share of something. |
| Mean NLL per token (and its fall with RL step) | Paper 1 Fig. 11 (JS percentiles of intermediate checkpoints on the final model's outputs). Wang Table 1 (entropy-position overlap over steps) | Different statistic; both papers also condition on the final model's outputs, so the text design (not the statistic) matches ours. In addition, Paper 1 Fig. 11a measures JS against the BASE (every percentile is 0 at step 0 and rises), i.e. distance from the start, whereas our NLL and `tokens_summary.json` JS/excess measure distance towards s2300: the trajectories run in opposite senses. Only Fig. 11b (Jaccard with the final divergent set) points towards the final model. | Text design comparable; statistics and direction not. |
| Thinking / answer split | None of the three papers splits segments. Closest: Paper 1 Fig. 26b (local JS by distance from the answer span) and Section 2.3, which reads the late rise in divergence as "answer formatting and termination behavior" (an interpretation, a different statistic) | Our answer segment has about 14k positions in 134 responses (2.5 % of all positions), so it is a small and noisy sample. | Not applicable. |
| 95 % bootstrap over responses | No intervals in any of the three papers (apart from the 3-run training spread in Paper 1 Table 2) | Ours is a response-level bootstrap; their single numbers hide sampling noise. | Their numbers cannot be used as intervals for ours. |

What this means for the write-up:
- The direction of our design (final model's samples, earlier checkpoints score them) matches Paper 1 Section 4.3 and Wang Section 4 Table 1, not Akgul (base text, RL scores).
- The papers study mostly maths RL from base models (Akgul's Qwen3-4B teacher is General-Reasoner, GRPO with a model verifier on multi-domain data; Wang Section 3 analyses Qwen3-8B, a post-trained thinking model, though its RL runs start from base models; Paper 1 also uses GPQA); we use a thinking model at several RL steps from a DPO start, with instruction following. Differences in sparsity numbers from theirs cannot be attributed to the method of measuring without matched runs.
- Any statement like "similar to the sparse footprint reported in the literature" should rest on argmax agreement or JS (which `tokens_summary.json` contains), not on the sampled-token top-1/top-5 shares or the nats concentration reported by this footprint.
- Open questions I did not resolve: whether Akgul's mean rank counts rank from 1 or 0 (text says "second most likely", mean 2.14-2.39, suggesting from 1; the auditor says 1-indexed); which decoding settings give Akgul's base pass@1 of 0.233 (Appendix A) against 0.298 (Table 3).
