Pass@k by thinking budget

One panel per model. Left to right is more test-time thinking; up is more problems solved with the same number of samples. A line that climbs means thinking longer pays. Most are flat.

Each point is one checkpoint sampled at one thinking budget: the share of a problem pool solved with k samples per problem (64 unless you change it), with the count written above it. The shaded band is the range of budgets the model was trained on. The dashed orange line is run 198, the ordinary untied model with no loops and no thoughts, after 4 RL rounds under the old harness, where it was scored on the same pool.

How to read these

  • A flat line means the thinking budget does nothing at that sample count.
  • A step up at the left, then flat means the smallest budget is too little and everything above it is equivalent. That is the looped models: 1 loop is behind, and nothing above about 4 adds more.
  • A peak inside the shaded band that falls away outside it means the model only works at the budgets it trained on. That is the fixed-2-loop control.
  • More loops are not free. A loop count of n runs 2 + 2n blocks per token against run 198's 6, so 8 loops costs three times as much per sample. Thoughts cost one extra position each, so those panels are near enough equal-cost left to right.
  • RL unsolved is each model's own list of RL targets it never solved, so that pool is comparable along a line, not between panels.
  • Per-line recurrence was only ever scored at 0 and 8 thoughts per line, on the whole dev pool at 64 samples, so those panels have two points and appear under "Whole dev".
Pools: textbook72 (72 textbook problems, 256 samples each), long dev (the 329 dev theorems with true length 10 or more, 64 samples), whole dev (all 1,108 dev theorems of length 7 or more, 64 samples; looped models were scored on it at 2 and 8 loops only), RL unsolved (128 samples).
