# Isolate learned stopping under a preserved generator and scorer

This proposed, unrun experiment follows the [reviewed recommendation](../reviews/next_stopping_experiment_judgment.md); it does not alter current registries. MAIN selection failed the strongest-baseline criterion, and full BCE damaged DEV generation. Ask whether count adds useful stopping information beyond constant confidence while preserving generation and proof ranking.

The learned object is a small stopping controller around a frozen proof model. A positive result would establish improved allocation under that fixed system. It would not show that the proof language model itself learned contextual generation, proof selection and stopping jointly, or establish adaptation across new tasks.

## Cheap diagnostic before the new stopping study

First apply the existing frozen-backbone 385-parameter critic to saved joint-policy DEV candidates, chosen before inspecting its reranking results. At fixed counts 1, 2, 4 and 8, compare native selection, this critic and oracle availability on exactly the same retained candidates; charge the critic's full backbone forwards. Keep generation unchanged. This diagnostic asks whether a preserved recognizer can recover selection loss in those pools. Its inputs have shifted from original-model proofs to adapted, context-conditioned proofs, so a negative result would limit this critic rather than all possible recognition methods. A positive result would identify recoverable selection loss, not repair later generation or establish useful stopping. Any component choices motivated by the diagnostic need fresh evaluation. This is a proposed DEV diagnostic, not a retrofit to the current TEST results; it does not replace the stopping experiment below.

## Fixed system and hypothesis

Keep the original r16 generator and the existing 385-parameter frozen-head proof scorer unchanged, with their exact checkpoint, head, tokenizer and public transition rendering hashes. Generate every attempt from a fresh original theorem state; previous attempts never enter the generator prefix. Score each proof independently from theorem plus public transitions. At count n, the incumbent is the highest-scoring available proof; integrate exact ties uniformly. The stopping policy observes only n and the incumbent's maximum sigmoid score. It receives no verifier result, error reason, reference proof, entropy, likelihood replay, future attempt or theorem-family identifier.

Use an eight-attempt cap and the existing sampling settings. The hypothesis is that a count-dependent confidence threshold improves held-out reward over the best constant confidence threshold, without sacrificing the original generator or changing the proof scorer. This tests stopping under a fixed system; it does not test joint proof-generation adaptation.

## Data separation

Prefer new independent stopping-training pools: 1,200 training, 400 development and 800 final-test theorems, balanced across the same two declared families for this first controlled comparison. Group by atom-erased connective skeleton before splitting. Exclude all current sprint theorems and pilot skeletons, known generator/scorer training exposure and public benchmark canonical matches. Do not inspect protected evaluation prompts. Freeze distributions and counts before new candidate outcomes; retain all-invalid, all-valid and overflow cases without outcome-based replacement. Constructed target certificates remain outside every model input.

The scorer is already frozen from the earlier 1,600-theorem training corpus. It must not be refitted using any stopping-training, development or final-test labels. Private validity labels from stopping-training pools may supervise stopping reward. Development outcomes diagnose implementation and freeze budget-matching mixtures; they do not retune any fitted stopping policy. Final-test labels open only after immutable method and public-pool seals. Preserve explicit split checks and candidate-ID joins.

If independent pools are unavailable, use five structural folds within stopping training and obtain every training score from a scorer fit without that theorem's fold. This distinct cross-fitted variant must declare every fold model and deployment scorer; it changes the score-generating system. In-sample predictions are not equivalent.

## Policy, optimization and controls

The constant-threshold control has a useful theoretical justification under an idealization. Suppose each future draw has an iid, known payoff \(Y\in[0,1]\), its payoff is observed reliably, the current best payoff is \(x\), old candidates remain available, and every draw costs the same \(c>0\). With any positive finite number of draws remaining, stopping is optimal exactly when \(g(x)=\mathbb E[(Y-x)_+]\le c\), with stopping chosen at equality. If \(g(x)>c\), one draw followed by stopping already improves value. If \(g(x)\le c\), every future incumbent \(z\ge x\) also satisfies \(g(z)\le c\); backward induction therefore makes further draws unhelpful. At zero remaining draws, stopping is compulsory. If \(c\ge\mathbb E[Y]\), stopping is optimal everywhere. Thus finite horizon alone does not require count-dependent thresholds in this idealization. A genuine expected-value advantage from count dependence must exploit departures such as latent theorem difficulty, belief updates, imperfect calibration, changing future distributions, or omitted state; an apparent finite-sample gain could also reflect noise or optimization error. These assumptions are not established for our proof scorer; this calculation motivates a control rather than predicts the result. [Astra independently checked the argument](../reviews/iid_stopping_control_review.md) during the sprint.

Learn seven thresholds, one for each count 1–7; stop when incumbent score reaches that count's threshold. Force stopping at eight. Acceptance is monotone in confidence at each count, but impose no across-count ordering. Use the existing confidence grid plus explicit always-stop/never-stop values. Optimize empirical expected correctness minus 0.03 times additional attempts over eight prospectively seeded candidate permutations per training theorem. Use deterministic coordinate ascent, starting from the training-optimal constant threshold, at most 20 full sweeps; ties prefer fewer attempts and then the fixed grid order. Publish the terminal schedule and all intermediate objectives. No MLP or representation fine-tuning is needed.

Compare against a constant confidence threshold and count-only stopping optimized over every legal count1–8, plus conventional fixed1/2/4/8 readouts and development-frozen mixtures. Outcome-independent count hazards are mixtures of fixed counts, so the training-optimal deterministic count suffices for expected reward. Fit adaptive, constant and count-only policies on the same TRAIN pools/orders and reward; none receives DEV checkpoint or hyperparameter selection. Also freeze mixtures matching development mean attempts and charged generation-plus-scoring inputs; report actual test mismatches instead of refitting weights on test. Charge scorer forwards and generator prefill, with the fixed-one scoring exemption. Token counts remain compute lower bounds; measure end-to-end wall time separately. Report accuracy, attempts, reward, family results, confidence/count strata and all hard-limit overrides. Retain the learned-feature threshold as a separate deployment reference with its own scoring costs; improvement within the frozen-head system does not establish the best overall system.

The positive criterion combines a test point-estimate reward gain of at least0.005 over the constant threshold with paired95% interval lower bounds above zero against constant and count-only controls. It does not establish a true gain exceeding0.005; that requires a lower bound above0.005. Fit one schedule per method. Three declared pool-generation seeds are evaluation replications on the same held-out theorems, not three independent fits. Report each replication, average within theorem, and bootstrap by family. Retain fixed-mixture contrasts; do not expand representations on this test set if the criterion fails.

Count may help because it corrects confidence after taking a maximum over noisy scores, or because it provides information about theorem difficulty. Either mechanism can support useful allocation under the stated reward criterion. A later mechanism control could fit incumbent validity from count and score, then choose a single posterior-confidence threshold under the same TRAIN reward protocol. If that validity estimate is monotone in score, a global threshold already induces count-specific score thresholds; it can express the same policy class. Such a comparison distinguishes fitting objectives and constraints, not necessarily expressiveness. Beating it would not by itself prove prediction of future improvement, and matching it would not negate a held-out allocation gain. No such calibrator has been fitted in this sprint.

## What would justify a broader meta-learning interpretation?

The above is ordinary outcome-supervised stopping generalization. A stronger learning-to-adapt study should define tasks, disjoint support/query outcomes, an adaptation mechanism and an across-task objective targeting post-adaptation performance. That objective may be explicit or implicit in episode rewards; an explicit outer-loop implementation is not a universal definition of meta-learning.

For this proposal, use many procedural task families varying connective/rule composition, branching, difficulty and continuation-value distributions, rather than atom renamings or two fixed templates. Hold out entire families and generation regimes as a strong transfer test beyond new theorem skeletons, while verifying usable original-generator success rates. Such family holdouts are a proposed stronger test, not a universal prerequisite.

For each unseen task, adapt a stopping schedule from a small support set and evaluate unseen query theorems. Compare against a global schedule, constant/count-only policies, and scratch adaptation with matched support labels and compute. Reproducible query gains under support/query isolation would support the specific claim of learned adaptation of compute allocation. In-context history or RL across ordinary theorems alone does not establish that claim; neither an outer objective nor novel family names is sufficient evidence by itself.
