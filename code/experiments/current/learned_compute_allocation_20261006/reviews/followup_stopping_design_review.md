# Concrete refinements to the unrun stopping follow-up

The proposed [follow-up](../selection/followup_stopping_design.md) appropriately freezes generation/ranking, separates stopping-training labels from scorer fitting, and treats cross-fitting as a distinct system. No new experiment is needed for this planning review. Four wording refinements would remove material ambiguities:

1. **Specify tuning and seed roles.** Replace “Give comparators the same tuning data and reward definition” with: “Fit each candidate stopping policy and each count-only/constant-confidence control on the same stopping-training pools and seeded orders. Prespecify whether DEV selects an optimization checkpoint or hyperparameters; offer the same declared selection opportunity to comparable controls, and freeze all choices before TEST. Separately declare whether the three pool-generation seeds create three independently fitted stopping systems or only repeated evaluation pools for one fitted system. Report each fitted system and average repeated outcomes within theorem before resampling.” The current training-optimal initialization is fine, but should not silently become a training-only final comparator against a DEV-selected learned schedule, or vice versa.

2. **Cover all count-only policies.** Replace “reward-trained count-only stopping” with: “a count-only policy optimized over every legal stop count 1–8 (equivalently, outcome-independent count hazards), while additionally showing conventional fixed1/2/4/8 readouts.” With a fixed generator/scorer and no observations beyond count, a randomized count policy is a mixture of fixed counts; the best deterministic count suffices for expected reward at a fixed cost. Restricting this comparator to 1/2/4/8 could misattribute the benefit of choosing count3/5/6/7 to useful confidence conditioning. Preserve matched-mixture controls separately.

3. **Clarify the effect-size criterion.** Replace the success sentence with: “The prespecified criterion combines a test point-estimate reward gain of at least 0.005 over the frozen constant threshold with paired 95% interval lower bounds above zero against both the constant-threshold and count-only controls. This does not establish that the true gain exceeds 0.005; that stronger claim would require an interval lower bound above 0.005.” Either criterion is defensible if chosen prospectively, but they should not be conflated.

4. **Avoid universal meta-learning requirements.** Replace the final two paragraphs' necessity framing with: “For a stronger learning-to-adapt follow-up, define distinct tasks, separated support/query outcomes, an adaptation mechanism and an across-task training objective that targets post-adaptation performance; the outer objective may be explicit or implicit in episode rewards. Evaluate on held-out tasks with support/query isolation and compare with the globally trained policy and scratch adaptation under matched labels/compute. Holding out entire proof-program families and generation regimes adds a stronger out-of-distribution transfer test; it is not a universal prerequisite for meta-learning. The claim here would be specifically learned adaptation of compute allocation, supported by reproducible query gains under that protocol.” Ordinary supervised stopping remains the correct description of the first proposed experiment. Merely requiring an explicit outer-loop implementation or unseen family labels is neither a general definition nor a sufficient empirical success criterion.

No changes to frozen methods, current registries, data or outcomes are implied. The first controlled experiment can remain small; the task-adaptation extension is a separate future study.
