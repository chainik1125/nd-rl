How much does RL raise each problem's solve rate?

One textbook72 problem per unit of height. The x axis is the change in log-odds of a Lean-checked proof per attempt, RL checkpoint against pretrain, both at 10,240 attempts and temperature 1. Right of the line, RL solves the problem more often.

RL loses no problem, and only the 3 h checkpoint lowers a rate. At 3 h, two of the 15 problems pretrain solves get worse: db298f46 falls from 23 to 7 successes (odds ×0.32) and a388b9fc from 9,468 to 9,280 (×0.79). At 5.4 h all 15 improve; the smallest gain is ×1.8.

The typical gain is large. On the problems pretrain solves, the median odds gain is ×15 at 3 h and ×18 at 5.4 h. The largest are on 8e48b958, 48 successes to 9,726 (×3,973) and 9,546, and 8f1e47f4, 1 success to 2,071 and 2,775.

Why log-odds and not a rate ratio. A ratio of rates hides gains near the ceiling. On 76875ce1 the rate goes from 0.835 to 0.970, a ratio of 1.16, while failures fall from 1,688 to 310 and the odds rise ×6.3. Log-odds treats the bottom and the top of the scale alike, and a problem's position does not depend on which side of 50% it starts.

How the zeros are handled. Each rate is smoothed as (successes + ½) / (attempts + 1), so a problem pretrain never solved sits as if pretrain had half a success. Its true gain is larger, which makes the hatched bars lower bounds. Problems neither model solves (30 at 3 h, 29 at 5.4 h) are left out; their smoothed change is zero by construction.
