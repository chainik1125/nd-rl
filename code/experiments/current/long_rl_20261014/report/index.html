What the curves say so far

Expert iteration works, and the bridge from short to long proofs holds

Both EI arms climb steadily. Untied EI went from about 0.10 to 0.55–0.60 rollout accuracy and pushed L* from 7 to 20–21 body lines in about 1.6 h of RL. The pretrained model has never seen a proof longer than 6 body lines. Run v1 had no short-to-long bridge in its pool and solved nothing.

Looped trails untied

Looped EI starts at about half the untied accuracy (0.05 vs 0.10) and stays behind, about 0.45 vs 0.58, with L* at 17 against 20–21. Its retries at 2, 4 and 8 loops add a few targets per round; the final dev scores will show whether that buys anything at test time.

GRPO is unstable and does not learn

  • Rollout accuracy saw-tooths between 0 and 0.15 and never climbs. Targets solved flatten at about 1,150 after 10 minutes and creep to about 1,400; EI is past 3,200.
  • In the worst rounds 80–100% of samples don't parse, and entropy spikes to 0.3–0.9 nats per token.
  • One round of updates can wipe out the policy. Seed 1, round 8 sampled at 0.099 accuracy; after that round's 27 gradient steps, round 9 sampled at 0.016, and two rounds later at 0.0008.
  • It recovers only partly each time, pulled back by the retained pretraining loss and the injected found proofs. That is the saw-tooth.

Likely cause: steps that are too large, with nothing holding the policy near where the rollouts came from

  • Optimizer. The GRPO update uses AdamW at lr 1e-4 on every parameter. EI's fine-tune uses Muon on the matrices and AdamW only on embeddings, norms and biases, as in pretraining. Adam rescales every step to about the same size, so small advantages (±1/8 to 7/8) don't make steps smaller.
  • No trust region. No PPO ratio clipping, one pass per round, no KL term to the pretrained model.
  • Mostly negative signal once it collapses. In collapsed rounds every usable group is an injected one: 1 found proof and 7 failing samples pushed down.

v3: GRPO relaunched with the fixes at 12:50 PDT

v2’s GRPO pods were stopped at 12:42 on Robbie’s call and chose the fixes. Same data, pretraining, target picking and budget; only the update changed.

  • Optimizer: EI's split, Muon on the matrices and AdamW on the rest, at a tenth of EI's fine-tune rates: Muon 3e-4, AdamW 3e-5.
  • Trust region: PPO clipped ratio against the sampling policy with Leon's asymmetric range [0.8, 1.28], and a dual clip at 3× for negative advantages. Old log-probs are scored once before each round's first step.
  • Temperature: the loss and the ratio use logits / 0.8, the distribution the rollouts were sampled from.
  • New diagnostics: clip fraction and mean ratio per round, and entropy and −log p at T = 0.8. At the sampling temperature these two are equal in expectation, so a gap means the sampler and trainer disagree. v2's collapsed rounds had −log p above entropy even at T = 1, which should not happen.

Reading the entropy numbers

  • Entropy is the policy's own next-token entropy at temperature 1, averaged over every proof token of the round's rollouts, before the update. Sampling runs at temperature 0.8, so the sampled distribution is sharper than the one measured.
  • Most tokens in a Fitch proof are forced: line numbers, separators, brackets, the formula an elimination rule must produce. The uncertainty sits in a few choices per line: which rule, which lines to cite, what to assume. A per-token mean of 0.07–0.09 nats is an average over a few uncertain tokens and many near-zero ones.
  • EI barely moves entropy because it fine-tunes on its own accepted samples. Those already have low loss under the policy (fine-tune loss 0.02–0.07), so each round sharpens slightly and adds new targets without reshaping the distribution.

Excess description length

How much does a checkpoint still have to learn to write a band's gold proofs, as opposed to having it already? Excess description length (EDL; Donoway, Joren, Roger & Leike 2026, arXiv 2601.04728) measures this in nats.

  • The game. Take a copy of the checkpoint and show it the band's n training proofs one at a time. Score each proof (−log p of its tokens, in nats) just before taking a gradient step on it. The sum of those scores is the prequential code length: what it costs to transmit the proofs to someone who holds the same checkpoint and learns along the way.
  • The excess. Subtract n × the final model's mean loss on n held-out proofs. What remains is the area between the online loss curve and the final loss line (left chart). It counts the nats that had to go into the weights, and that generalise.
  • Reading it. Small means the model already had the capability and a few examples brought it out: elicitation. Large means it had to learn. Memorising training proofs doesn't count, because the floor is measured on held-out proofs. Ambiguity that never resolves, such as several valid proofs of one theorem, sits in both terms and cancels.
  • In quant terms. It is the cumulative regret of the online learner against its own final predictor, with the final predictor scored out of sample.

Setup: edl/edl.py. For each body-line band, holdout gold proofs are split by key hash into n train and n test (n = 64; 22 for 7–9, 32 for 35–49, 48 for 50+). The learner is the RL fine-tune's: Muon plus AdamW, one proof per step, constant rate, at 0.1, 0.3, 1 or 3 × the fine-tune's rates, with two data orders. Each band uses the multiple with the smallest online code length and adds ln 4 nats for having tried four (Leon's convention). Rates whose held-out loss rose more than 5% over the second half are excluded: at 1× and 3× the last steps sometimes destabilise the model, and subtracting a worse final loss made EDL negative. Scoring is unaugmented, at 8 loops for looped. Values are nats per training proof (EDL / n), so bands with different n compare.

Caveats. These are gold proofs from the long-proofs generator, and RL never trains on them. Part of each value is learning that generator's style rather than the ability to prove, which shifts every checkpoint by a similar amount: differences between checkpoints mean more than absolute values. The learner is our choice, so the values don't compare with the paper's.

What it shows (29 checkpoints, scored 18:25–18:55 PDT; v5 not yet)

  • Pretrained EDL grows with proof length: about 11–21 nats per proof at 7–9 body lines, 35–74 at 20–24 and 136–228 at 50+.
  • RL removes most of it on bands it solves. Final EI checkpoints are at 1–3 nats per proof at 7–9 and 9–19 at 20–24, five to ten times less.
  • It also drops where RL solves nothing. At 35–49, which RL never solves on dev, EI goes from 55–124 to 19–41, so RL put in information that carries to lengths it can't yet prove. Part of this could be the generator's style, which the caveat above covers.
  • Arms differ. At 35–49 and 50+, GRPO ends higher than EI: 58–70 against 19–41, and 135–150 against 63–115. Looped EI ends lowest at 50+ (63–65 against untied's 85–115), though it ties untied on dev.
  • Over RL (v4): 7–9 collapses within the first 5 rounds (21 to 2 in seed 0, 13 to 2 in seed 1). 35–49 falls steadily, from 113 to 81, 57, 43, 41, 36, 34 and then 19 at final in seed 0.
  • Noise: one fixed split and two data orders. Differences under about 5–10 nats per proof at 34 lines and below are within noise, e.g. v4 seed 1 at 25–34 jumps between 4 and 33 across rounds.

  • Pretraining: Dan's 155k cap-6 file, with distractor premises drawn so each record has between its own count and 10 premises, and atom relabelling across 10 atoms. 600 s on one A40.
  • RL pool: 18,439 Lean-checked theorems of 7 to 80 body lines, 3–10 premises: the long-proofs set (certified ≥ 20 by exhaustive search or the signature bound), Dan's ladder targets, and the 35–80-line generator output. Dev 843, holdout 823, split by canonical key.
  • Budgets: 3 h of RL GPU time, Lean time not charged; 6.5 h hard stop per pod.
  • Cost: 6 A40 pods at $0.59/h, about $3 each, about $18 total.

Run v1: nothing solved

The first launch at 09:05 PDT used only the long theorems as RL targets. The pretrained model solved 0 of 677 dev theorems at 64 samples, and every RL round was all-fail, so nothing could learn. Greedy decoding still solved 39 of 40 training records, so pretraining itself was fine. The gap from 6 to 7+ body lines with 3–10 premises was too big a jump. v2 added Dan's ladder targets and a short new-family bridge, and every arm solved targets in its first round.
