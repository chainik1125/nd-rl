# Card: schema acquisition — a capability is a family, acquired as a whole

Families N / T. Slug `schema-acquisition`. Notation: `_FRAME.md`.

## 1. Definition, formally

- **A capability is a theorem family F** whose members share a proof schema: the 19 textbook schemata of `rl_targets`
  / transfer (40 + 40 instances each, generated by renaming and substituting formulas; e.g. `excluded_middle` = A ∨ ¬A
  with A any formula), or a length or shape class.
- **Family solve rate** r_θ(F; k) = the share of F's members that θ solves at budget k (or mean p_θ over members).
  Report it separately on **trained-on** members (`rl_targets`) and **held-out** members (transfer; holdout250's
  members are a subset).
- **Acquisition curve:** r over pretraining checkpoints and RL rounds. The ladder logs give it per round at k 32
  (round r samples the model of round r − 1; round 1 = pend).
- **Step-level novelty** (a "new move"): the schema's key step (e.g. `Classical.byContradiction` closing a ¬¬-goal,
  the DN rule) is in the base's step-level support at the relevant states, π_B(step | state) ≥ ε, or it is not. This
  separates "new composition of old moves" from "new move".

## 2. Decision rule

| verdict | rule |
|---|---|
| **created at family level** | base r_B(F_heldout; K) ≤ 0.05 and RL r_R(F_heldout; k_eval) ≥ 0.5, **on held-out members**; the base's figure is checked at the budget K of `passk-budget` |
| **elicited at family level** | r_B(F; K) > 0.05 at the budget (the base already solves some members) and RL raises it to ≥ 0.5 |
| **neither** | RL's held-out rate < 0.5 |

The step-level tag (new move vs new composition) is reported beside each family verdict. Per-theorem membership in the
agreement matrix: every holdout250 member of a created family.

## 3. Null or floor

- The family is the null against "lucky" single solves. Random weights solve no member of any family, so a family-level
  rate is immune to "some k solves one instance by chance".
- The schema's per-instance probability can still be tiny for the base at large K. Check the base at the budget, not
  only at k 32.

## 4. How to compute it here

**Training shares, free (`cd_schema.py --keystep`, `out/schema_c12_keystep.txt`).** Per EI round, the share of each
family's 40 trained-on `rl_targets` members (classical families: key-step members only) with ≥ 1 success in 32
attempts; round 1 samples pend. The full table is in §9 and REPORT §3.6 (excluded middle 0.00 → 0.00 / **0.95** / 0.26
at r16; Peirce 0.00 → 0.00 / 0.27 / **1.00**; classical negated-conditional 0.05 / 0.00 / 0.00 → 1.00 / 0.65 / 1.00;
distribution 0.00 → 0.97 / 1.00 / 0.93). These are 32-attempt base reads, far below any budget.

**The decision rule, at the budget (`cd_part3.py`, `schema`).** Held-out members = each family's holdout250 members
(2–6 per family), key-step only. The base reaches a member if p̂ ≥ 1 / K_eval-set over all its standard-cap attempts
(J2 gives every hard member ≥ 16,896, the stage-A zeros 66,304); RL's share is solved at k 256 on the draw.

  seed floors are uninformative at these sizes.
- Excluded middle on s1 is the robust case: base 0 / 66,560 on each of the six members; r16 also solves 36 / 39 of
  the classical-only held-out transfer instances (s0 0, s2 13).
- **Teachability (J4, J6, J6b; `out/lem.txt`).** Four demonstrations of excluded middle (s1's RL proofs of other
  instances) take pend from 0 / 39 held-out instances to 36 / 33 / 36; sixteen to 37 / 34 / 37. A knockout pretrained
  without double negation learns it as fast (J6b gain difference +0.05 / −0.01 / −0.01): **teachable, not latent**
  (`elicit-finetune`).

## 5. Sensitivity

- **k:** the ladder's k 32 per round is small. At the budget, pend reaches 2 of the 3 held-out `dist_and_over_or`
  members on every seed (e.g. `la_transfer_428` 1 / 9 / 2 successes in 17,152), so the family is elicited.
- **Thresholds** 0.05 and 0.5: held-out families have 2–6 members, so one member moves a rate by 0.17–0.5; s1's
  Peirce (1 of 2) sits exactly on the line. With all members (no key-step restriction) `demorgan_nand_to_or` sat on
  the line at 0.47–0.57; key-step only, it is 0.00.
- **Decoding:** plain only in the ladder logs; guided would raise rates.
- **Representation:** families are defined on statements, so they are interface-free. Rates are not.
- **Renaming and substitution** are what define a family. That is the point: a capability survives renaming.
- **Noise:** 40 members give binomial SE ≤ 0.08 per family rate. Seeds differ qualitatively (LEM on 1 of 3), so the
  verdict must be per seed and reported as m / 3.

## 6. Failure modes

- **Family membership is an artefact of the generator.** A family-level rate measures "acquired the generator's
  template", which may be one memorised rewrite.
- **Trained-on vs held-out.** RL trains on the `rl_targets` members, so only held-out members test the capability
  (transfer).
- **The base at k 32 is a very small budget.** "Created at k 32" can be "elicited at 10³" (as for single theorems).
- **Per-seed creation (1 of 3 seeds for LEM)** means "RL can create it", not "RL creates it". A population claim needs
  more seeds.

## 7. Relations

- The family-level version of `passk-budget` and `reliability`.
- Held-out members make it `transfer-invariance`.
- New-move vs new-composition is `composition`.
- Demonstration-elicitability of a family is `elicit-finetune` (J4).
- Pretraining exposure of the family's moves is `out-of-data-novelty` and `causal-ablation` (J6).
