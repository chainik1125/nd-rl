   absorb to learn it, and invited pushback. The learned numbers are kalmar-pretrain's KAL-D (9,560,832 params,

   judged.
   - **The program is a few kilobits.** The shared-lemma algorithm alone is 5,443 bits of binary λ-calculus and 864 B
     of compressed Python, and 6,859 λ-calculus bits with parsing and rendering. It is the largest of nine calibration

     operations. Every program reproduces the builder on the reviewer's fresh sample.

     runs give 1.1–1.5 Mbit, of which 52–73 % is the first block's uniform code.

     RASP program's effective depth ρ +0.85, in-scope lookups +0.82; 15 types), not by the length of the code that
     emits the step (−0.08 given frequency; within the 9 leaf types −0.80, or −0.68 given frequency). The run
     recommends, and the review accepts if the residual is named, stating "how much there is to learn" as a
     learner-relative excess description length under a stated protocol, plus a per-step demand profile.

     seeds), but that subformula is the formula the step writes, so search and copying are not separated.

   every ladder whose fine-tune replays pretraining data (`next_steps.md` 2). Question 4 asks a related question about
   σ.

   cumulative RL / pretraining FLOPs, from an arm at ≈ 10⁻³ of Stage-1 (Yue's regime) to the ladders' ≈ 10
   (rl-compute-share's next measurement). Knockout s1's late member-level reach hints that it does.

   subformula cuts KAL-D-like models' opener-step error 94 %, but the shown subformula is the formula the step
   writes. The review's POS (pointer only) and DECOY arms separate the two (≈ $2). The answer decides what a
   "per-step demand profile" should count, and the pretraining-vs-RL bits study plans to split bits by that demand
   (kalmar-complexity's `next_steps.md` 4).
4. **Does K12 supply the reductio form, or does RL-time replay re-supply it?** The full arm replays 20,000 pretraining
   records per EI round, σ templates included. A full-arm ladder whose replay excludes σ separates the two.
   rl-compute-share's implication 3 also asks for a no-replay arm: replay is continued pretraining, and it inflates
   the as-run RL share.
5. **Is the premise-count wall only about the premise lines?** The K12 control solves 177–191 of 200 four- and
   five-premise problems at k 32 once the environment writes the premise lines, and 0–26 as is (kalmar-pretrain).
   Letting the environment apply premise lines would cost ≈ $1 in re-reads (executor's estimate). peer-rl-review
   recorded the wall for Charles's problems.

   pretraining holds 6 vs 2 free and 16,658 vs 1,545 template-only reductio proofs.
