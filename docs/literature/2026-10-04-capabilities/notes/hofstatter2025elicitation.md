# The Elicitation Game: which elicitation techniques recover hidden capabilities

Paper: [@hofstatter2025elicitation] (Hofstätter, van der Weij, Teoh, Djoneva, Bartsch, Ward; ICML 2025)
Source: arXiv 2502.02180v3 (HTML rendering read: abstract, Sec. 1-6, App. A.4, D; other appendices skimmed)

## Learnings

- **Formal definitions (Sec. 3.3).** For a score function s on context-response pairs with r ~ p_θ(·|c), "We say
  an LM exhibits capability ŝ = s({(c,r)}) when it achieves that score on a given benchmark". "A latent
  capability s̃ is one that a model exhibits with low probability by default, but can be revealed through small
  changes to the context, activations, or weights. Such changes include few-shot prompting or minor parameter
  updates requiring less than 1% of the original training cost (Anthropic, 2023)" (Sec. 3.3).
- **Motivation.** "previously latent capabilities have been elicited from models, sometimes long after initial
  release" (Abstract; Sec. 1, e.g. chain-of-thought).
- **Model organisms.** Password-locked models (after Greenblatt et al. 2024) and a new password-locked +
  circuit-broken organism, which penalises the original activations on no-password inputs (Sec. 3.4, losses
  L_CB, L_retain). The circuit-broken organism is "more robust to elicitation techniques than standard
  password-locked models" (Abstract), but "circuit-breaking was very fragile to training hyperparameters"
  (Sec. 4.1).
- **Results.** MCQA (WMDP, MMLU): combining N-shot prompting and prefilling "always elicits the capabilities"
  on WMDP (Sec. 4.1); steering is weaker. Code (APPS, Gemma-2-9b-it): "only fine-tuning can elicit the hidden
  capabilities of our novel model organism" (Abstract); multi-turn prompting peaks at "6.7%" (Sec. 4.3); "Both
  steering methods fail at elicitation, with a highest score of 2%" (Sec. 4.3). "Anti-refusal training elicits
  the full hidden code-generation capabilities of both our model-organisms" (Sec. 4.3), although its training
  data (harmful requests with affirmative replies) is unrelated to the capability: "neither the requests nor
  responses need to be related to the capability that we want to elicit" (Sec. 3.5).
- **Why open-ended tasks are harder to elicit ("target space").** MCQA has four reasonable tokens; for code "It
  seems more difficult to elicit correct distributions for tasks with an increased breadth of plausible tokens
  at each step and an increased total number of tokens required" (Sec. 5).
- **Recommendation.** "if possible, fine-tuning should be the method of choice to improve the trustworthiness of
  capability evaluations" (Abstract); evaluators "should at least have fine-tuning access (and use it)" (Sec. 5).
- **Elicitation data budget.** 25 % of each MCQA dataset trains elicitation techniques (Sec. 3.1); for APPS
  "1000 to train elicitation techniques" (Sec. 3.1). SFT demonstrations for APPS were generated by the unlocked
  model because dataset solutions did not fit the harness format (App. D.1).
- **Limitations stated.** "It seems likely that at least some elicitation methods lead to substantially better
  results with increased resources" (Sec. 5); organisms may not resemble naturally hidden capabilities (Sec. 5).
  Suggested follow-up: "Comparing anti-refusal training to fine-tuning on random noise might reveal why
  anti-refusal training works" (Sec. 6).

## Evidence and limitations

- Ground truth again by construction (locked organisms). Effect sizes per model are in App. A tables (I checked
  only the text numbers quoted above). Single benchmark per format; temperature 0 throughout (Sec. 3.5).
- Organism training degrades the capability itself: Gemma's "original APPS performance of 35.8% could not be
  recovered with only the password" (Sec. 4.3) — so "actual capability" is measured against the password
  ceiling, not the original model.
- The 1 % budget is quoted from Anthropic (2023) without independent justification (Sec. 3.3).

## Connections and questions

- **Definition offered:** exhibited capability = score of default samples; latent capability = a score the model
  reaches only after a *small* change to context, activations or weights, small meaning few-shot prompting or
  < 1 % of original training cost. The capability is then the maximum score over an admissible elicitation set.
- **New vs better access:** decided by a cost budget (< 1 %) plus construction. One result offers a sharper,
  budget-free criterion: anti-refusal training recovers full code performance with training data that carries
  no task information. An elicitation whose data contains no information about the task cannot have *taught*
  the task, so recovery under such an elicitation certifies prior possession. This information criterion is
  stronger than a compute budget.
- **Null / floor:** chance level is reported (25 % MCQA, 0 % APPS); no discussion of random weights. Their
  proposed control (fine-tuning on random noise, Sec. 6) is the right negative control for "does this
  elicitation create?".
- **Transfer to our setting:** our elicitation menu maps onto theirs: prefilling ≈ forcing the first proof
  steps or guided sampling (environment-checked steps), steering ≈ activation steering (untested), SFT on demos ≈
  minutes of fine-tuning on reference proofs, anti-refusal ≈ fine-tuning on *task-irrelevant* data. Proposed
  measure: fine-tune pend for a fixed budget on (a) proofs of theorems disjoint from the target set (e.g. other
  schemata, or the base's own accepted proofs of easy theorems), (b) a matched amount of shuffled/noise
  targets (their random-noise control), (c) reference proofs of the target theorems (positive). Target-set gains
  under (a) beyond (b) are access gains carrying no target information; RL gains on targets in excess of (a)
  must come from information injected by verifier selection on the targets. Cost: minutes per fine-tune on one
  GPU plus a k = 256 read per arm (pod time). Failure mode: the "target space" argument predicts that weak
  elicitors under-recover on long proofs, so a negative result under (a) is weak evidence of absence; and
  "disjoint" theorems still share rules, so (a) can teach rule usage.
- Related notes: greenblatt2024passwordlocked.md, vanderweij2024sandbagging.md, deeb2024unlearning.md
  (fine-tuning on a disjoint split as the recoverability test).
