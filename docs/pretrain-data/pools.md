- **k12_minimal** (idea B1): `minimal_k12.py` re-proved every K12 theorem with the fork's minimal-proof search (bound =
  one line fewer, 3 s each, 85 s for 155,000 rows with 4 workers). 85,244 proofs got shorter, 55,665 were already minimal,
  14,091 two-line rows untouched. 374,537 lines removed, a third of the pool. Of the 84,545 long-mode rows (7–12 lines),
  only 9,361 were minimal: **K12's long half is 89 % padding**, almost all of it the lazy-premise case split (∨E over a
  premise disjunction the goal does not need). Mean length falls from 7.0 to 4.6 and the ∨E share of rule uses from
  14 % to 3 %. The pool now has almost no proofs over 9 lines, so it also removes the length signal that Dan's cap-12
  result may rest on; `k12_minimal_joins10` puts some length back with real content.
- **joins** (idea A11): `joins.py`, 71 templates in nine families (↔-elimination, ∧E-then-→E, nested ∧E, →E chains,
  negation, ↔-introduction, nested ∨E, four premises, depth 3), each filled 300 ways with small formulas over P Q R S,
  proved by the minimal-proof search (bound 14, 5 s) and kept only if the minimal proof contains the template's join:
  14,869 instances → 12,316 proved (1,390 bypassed the join, 1,163 timed out, mostly ↔-introduction with compound fills)
  → 12,295 after the audit (8 sealed twins, 13 evaluation twins removed). 23 % have ≥ 4 premises, 7 % depth ≥ 3, 15 %
  contain a non-degenerate ↔.

  406 classes → 383 after the audit (14 sealed twins and 9 test-split twins removed; 45 twins of seed-set problems kept
  and tagged). Small, so its slices are filled with atom permutations (up to 24 per class) and then repeats.
- Mixes replace a random 1 % or 10 % of K12 (1,550 / 15,500 rows) so that record count and Stage-1 wall-clock stay
  equal. The 1 % slices barely move pool-level rates, by design: Dan's pattern dial says 10⁻⁴ is enough for RL to

## Data screens: reading (2026-10-07)

Validation set: 224 seed-split problems with reference proofs, 58 unsolved by every evaluated model, 305 needed
cells (170 without the premise count). All three screens ran on all 27 pools in about 10 s per pool.

- **The predictor screen (J3) fails its negative control and is withdrawn as a loss proxy.** `k12_wrong100` scores
  *better* than K12 on perplexity (14.8 vs 21.6), median step loss (2.14 vs 2.19) and surprise share (0.207 vs 0.238).
  Corrupting rule labels flattens the pool's rule distribution, and a flatter count model is less overconfident on

## New pools: reading (2026-10-07)

Built on the laptop's CPU in under 20 minutes of wall-clock (the longest step, `compose`'s minimality check, 80 s with
3 workers); every proof checked by the pinned verifier, every new theorem audited by renaming class against the 412
evaluation problems and the sealed 72 (twins counted, removed, never listed). 21 raw records read across the seven sources,
plus before/after pairs of the closure re-proving.

- **closure** (A2 / H1): 59,984 sub-theorems, 24,432 from `k12_minimal` and 35,552 from the depth-3 side pool, after
  removing 7 sealed twins and 9 test-split twins. The depth-3 source is raw generator output, so its sub-proofs carried
  the lazy-premise case split: a 150-row sample of its ≥ 5-line records was shortenable by `minlen` in 98 cases (the
  `k12_minimal` source: 3 of 150). The builder now re-proves that source minimally (17,245 of 32,439 got shorter). The
  yield is short: 3–6 lines for 56,227 of 59,984, nothing above 11, and only 157 records with ≥ 4 premises, because K12's
  own proofs have ≤ 3 premises and depth ≤ 2, so an enclosing assumption rarely adds a fourth. It is *not* a 4-premise
  source. What it adds is mid-piece coverage (44 / 122 hard pieces, 11 / 89 unknown pieces at a 10 % slice, against
  K12's 33 / 0) and, like `k12_minimal`, an uncited premise in 1.5 % of rows (the minimal proof drops a premise).
- **compose** (A4 / H15): 14,010 verified splices over every minimal K12 row of ≤ 8 lines as the lemma side (9,704 found no usable partner: the lemma was
  only re-wrapped by ∧I / ∨I, or the premises collided), of which only **3,358 are minimal**; 10,636 had a shorter proof,
  because the lemma is usually cheaper to derive in place than to prove and cite. 24 % have ≥ 4 premises, mean 8.0 lines,
  →E←→E 4.8 and ¬E←→E 4.0 per 1,000 at a 10 % slice (K12 2.6 / 1.8); the slice is 3,358 sources + 12,142 permutations.
- **recover** (B3 / H3): 33,000 records (11 rows offered no dead move); ANDI 12,293, ∨I 17,110 (the fallback when the

## Pool-to-pool similarity: reading (2026-10-07)

Source: `data/pretrain_data/screens/pool_similarity.json` (34 pools, one pass each; no seeds involved, the measures are deterministic). Noise floor on the combined distance is 0.033 at K12's size and 0.069 at 15k.

- **Three families, not 34 pools.** At cut 0.10 the earlier pools fall into (i) a K12 core of 17 pools that are K12 with ≤ 10 % of rows replaced or only the surface changed (substitution, distractors, 1 % slices, the ↔ dial up to 1e-2, the size subsets), all within 0.06 of K12; (ii) the minimal family (`k12_minimal`, `_distract10`, `_joins10`) at 0.48–0.49 from K12 and 0.05–0.16 from each other; (iii) a tail of singletons that each move one marginal: the 10–30 % slices (`joins10`, `sub10`, `aux10`, `dial_1e1`, `tail10/30`, `wrong100`) and `k12_500k`. The take-home cap-6 set is nearest the minimal family (0.36) and 0.53 from K12: the cap-6 generator output looks more like de-padded K12 than like K12.
- **Where the new seven land.** `dual1` (0.027) and `closure10` (0.059) sit inside the K12 core: at 1 % and 10 % they are indistinguishable from K12 on skeleton and shape, and their novelty is only in the theorem set (closure: 10 % of classes new to K12, Jaccard 0.82). `multi10` (0.092), `compose10` (0.128) and `recover20` (0.136) form a loose band with `sub10`/`joins10` at 0.12–0.14: the same distance from K12 as a 10 % textbook slice, but for different reasons (`multi10` and `recover20` share 100 % of K12's theorem classes and differ only in proofs; `compose10`'s 10 % slice is ~3.4k new classes × permutations). `shape` (0.41) and `balance` (0.52) are the farthest pools from K12 after cap-6, and each other's and `tail30`'s nearest neighbours (0.26–0.31): on these views they are the depth-tail idea pushed to the whole pool, with 13–23 % of K12's classes retained.
- **What the views disagree on.** `distract*` and `subst*` are at 0.000 on both skeleton views (they do not touch the proof) and differ only on shape through the premise count; `wrong*` is at 0.000 on shape (prompt and line count unchanged) and differs on skeleton, so the negative control is visible to the skeleton views and invisible to the shape view. `minimal` is far from K12 on all three (0.42–0.53): removing padding changes the trigram, bigram and length distributions at once, more than any new pool does. So "similar to K12" needs the view named: `recover20` is as far from K12 on shape (0.17) as `distract10`, for the opposite reason (lines added, not premises).
- **Reading against the data screens.** The two pools the screens rank highest on coverage and distance (`shape`, `balance`) are also the two farthest from K12 and nearest `tail30`; the screens cannot tell whether that is the matching or the depth, and neither can this table (`tail30` at 0.26 is their closest earlier pool but still far above the floor). A training comparison `shape` vs `tail30` would settle it; on data alone they are different pools.

## Checkpoint screens: reading (2026-10-07)

Frozen `scale-d384-p1200_s0` (Robbie's K12 Stage-1, 9.56M), 24 pools with new slices, 3,000 records for loss and 160
prompts × 32 samples for pass@k per slice, about 4 min per pool on the laptop. Reference: K12 training rows loss 0.171,
pass@1 0.76, pass@32 0.89; held-out head 0.170 / 0.69 / 0.86.

- **The loss ratio alone mislabels the pools; pass@32 is the discriminator.** K12's loss is already 0.17 per token, so
  most slices land within 0.8–1.5× of it whatever their difficulty. The clearest case is the E8 dial: its ↔-elimination
  proofs have *lower* loss than K12's own (0.135, ×0.79) because they are four short formulaic lines, yet the base
  solves only 6 % of them per sample and 26–29 % within 32. Low loss given the proof, no ability to find it: exactly
  Shen's "surfaced, not amplified" signature, and the reason the dial is worth running. Regimes are therefore defined on
  pass@32 with the loss ratio as a secondary flag.
- **Far (pass@32 < 0.1): `k12_aux10`.** Loss 0.78 (×4.6), pass@32 0.00. The 13–24-line ∧/→ chains are an alien format
  to the K12 model: it has never produced a proof of that length or shape. Easy to learn from scratch, invisible to
  continued pretraining unless the slice is large; the only pool in this regime.
- **Edge (0.1 ≤ pass@32 < 0.8), the regime where RL acts (Zhang I1):** the join slices (`k12_joins1/10`,
  `k12_minimal_joins10`: pass@1 0.05–0.10, pass@32 0.14–0.20, loss ×1.4), the dial arms (0.25–0.29), `k12_pairs1`
  (0.33), `k12_spice1` (0.33), the sub-problem slices (0.51–0.54), the distractor slices (0.54–0.58) and the depth
  tails (0.61–0.66). Within the edge, the joins and the dial are nearest to "far": the base almost never produces the
  ∧E-then-→E shape even when the formulas are small. The distractor result is the cleanest single number of the
  screen: on K12 problems with one or two unused premises added, pass@32 falls from 0.89 to 0.55, so **premise
  selection costs the base model a third of its solves**, which is the 4-premise cell's failure in miniature. The depth
  tails are less far than expected: two thirds of depth-3 problems are solved within 32 samples, so depth 3 is reachable
  at base and the tail's job is to raise the rate, not to create the ability.
- **Redundant (pass@32 ≥ 0.8, loss ≤ 1.5×):** `cap6` (0.99; the K12 model subsumes the take-home set), the 500k
  generator rows (0.975, ×0.97: more of the same content teaches the base nothing it does not know, so E7's value is
  only in epochs), `k12_subst30/100` (0.81–0.85, ×0.9: substituted formulas are *easier* for the model than K12's, so the
  width-generalisation hypothesis has no leverage at this width) and `k12_minimal` (0.875 on its K12 problems; loss
  ×0.64).
- **The minimal proofs are the lowest-loss thing in the table apart from cap6.** The base model assigns K12's own
  shortened proofs a loss of 0.109 against 0.171 for the padded originals it was trained on: the lazy ∨E case split is
  the hardest-to-predict part of K12, and removing it removes noise rather than content. This is the first evidence from
  the model side that the padding is a defect, and it raises the prior on E3 and E6.
- **The wrong pools: 11.6 % of rows cannot be encoded by lean_seq at all** (rule-label swaps that have no Lean
  rendering; `enc` 0.883). A lean_seq Stage-1 on `k12_wrong*` would have to drop those rows or crash, so the pool needs a
  filter step before E14 runs, and the "100 % wrong" arm is in fact 88 %. Loss ×2.1 on the rows that encode: the model
  finds one-error proofs clearly less likely than correct ones, which is a sign the base has learned validity, not only
  format. Pass@k here is on K12's problems and says nothing about the slice.
- **Retention.** No slice removes a cell the base depends on: the lost cells at the 10 % dose are 0–5 per pool, all
  with ≤ 6 K12 records to begin with; at 30 % the distractor and tail slices take half of 10–12 small cells (e.g.
  ∨I←→I at depth 2, 30 of 58). The 100 % pools displace everything and the column is meaningless for them.

**What changes after this screen.** Two pools fall: the substitution pools (redundant at base, more tokens for no new
shapes) and the auxiliary pool (far, and its format has no overlap with the textbook shapes). One rises: the distractor
pools now have a measured base-model deficit to close. The join pools, the dial and the pairs sit at the near end of
the edge, which is where a slice is most likely to matter and also where it is most likely to need more than 10⁻⁴ of
the pool (Gu I2). The recommendation in the data-screens reading stands, with `k12_distract10` added to the second
tier and `k12_subst*` and `k12_aux10` moved to "run only if a ladder is spare".
