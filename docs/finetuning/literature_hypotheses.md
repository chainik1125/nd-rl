## 2. Literature read for this note (2025–2026)

Elicitation and description length:
- Donoway, Joren, Roger, Leike, *Excess Description Length of Learning Generalizable Predictors*, [arXiv 2601.04728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04728): EDL = prequential codelength − n·L_test(θ*); random labels give 0; score answer tokens only; hold the algorithm fixed; elicitation shows EDL per example falling with n, teaching can rise.
- Donoway, Joren, Deweese, Perez, Schulman, Roger, Leike, *Bits That Count*, [ICML 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/donoway26a.html): elicitation needs "orders of magnitude less information than teaching"; "a few bits in a single example can unlock large fractions" of maximum performance; EDL predicts when LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning.
- Morris, Mireshghallah, Ibrahim, Mahloujifar, *Learning to Reason in 13 Parameters*, [arXiv 2602.04118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04118): TinyLoRA (a trainable vector times fixed random projections inside each matrix's SVD basis); Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct reaches 91.8 % GSM8K with 13 parameters under RL (full fine-tune 91.7 %); SFT at 13 parameters 83 %; "SFT requires 100–1000× larger updates"; binary reward carries at most k·H(R) bits per prompt; no random-subset baseline; Llama barely moves below 5 parameters.
- Schulman et al., *LoRA Without Regret*, [Thinking Machines 2025](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/): rank-1 LoRA matches full fine-tuning for RL, "about one bit per episode"; SFT needs rank that grows with the dataset.
- Khan, Tafveez, Bhatti, *Plasticity vs Rigidity*, [arXiv 2601.06677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06677): on ≤ 1.5B models rank 8 fails and rank 256 works for RLVR on a micro-budget; math-aligned models collapse (rigidity near an optimum).
- Greenblatt, Roger, Krasheninnikov, Krueger, *Stress-Testing Capability Elicitation With Password-Locked Models*, [NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19550); Hofstätter et al., *The Elicitation Game*, [ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02180); Le, Tan, Williams-King, *Reference-Grafting*, [arXiv 2608.29458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29458): a few demonstrations fine-tune a locked capability back; additive steering fails, setting a coordinate to a reference value works because the lock is a thresholded gate.
- Wen et al., *Unsupervised Elicitation of Language Models*, [arXiv 2506.10139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10139): labels from internal coherence match gold supervision.
- Venhoff, Arcuschin, Torr, Conmy, Nanda, *Base Models Know How to Reason, Thinking Models Learn When*, [arXiv 2510.07364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07364): steering vectors plus a "when" classifier close much of the base-to-thinking gap without weight updates.

What RL changes in the weights:
- Mukherjee, Yuan, Hakkani-Tür, Peng, *RL Finetunes Small Subnetworks*, [NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11711): 5–30 % of parameters change, updates nearly full rank, subnetworks overlap across seeds and algorithms, cause is near-on-policy data.
- Zhu et al., *The Path Not Taken: RLVR Provably Learns Off the Principals*, [arXiv 2511.08567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08567): RLVR updates avoid principal directions, SFT targets them.
- Shenfeld, Pari, Agrawal, *RL's Razor*, [ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04259): forgetting is set by KL(fine-tuned ‖ base) on the new task; on-policy RL picks the KL-closest solution.
- Zhao et al., *Echo Chamber*, [COLM 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07912); Shao et al., *Spurious Rewards*, [arXiv 2506.10947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10947); Wang et al., *RL with One Training Example*, [NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20571): RL amplifies pretraining behaviours; random rewards give Qwen-Math +21 of +29 points through the clipping bias; one example gives +37 points with post-saturation generalisation.
- Chu et al., *SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes*, [ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17161); Matsutani et al., *RL Squeezes, SFT Expands*, [ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21128); Yang et al., *Beyond Reasoning: RL Unlocks Parametric Knowledge*, [arXiv 2605.07153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07153): RL moves tail answers into the greedy set; examples never produced in 128 pre-RL samples give 83 % of the gain.
- Wang et al., *Beyond the 80/20 Rule*, [NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01939): gradients on the 20 % high-entropy forking tokens suffice.

The pass@k debate:
- Yue et al. [arXiv 2504.13837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837); Wu et al., *The Invisible Leash?*, [arXiv 2507.14843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14843); Yao et al., *Shrinkage, Expansion, or Both? A Two-Stage Dynamic View*, [arXiv 2510.04028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04028): stage 1 exploitation narrows, stage 2 (long training) widens when a rarely sampled optimal token finally gets positive advantage.
- Yuan et al., *Diversity Collapse via Overtraining*, [arXiv 2606.15455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15455): one observed success saturates a problem for high-k pass@k, so most RLVR updates are overtraining; restricting updates to zero-success problems lifts pass@256 above the base.
- Cai et al., *Curriculum RL Beyond the Base Model*, [arXiv 2606.22317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22317): locate the boundary with pass@k, teacher-guide at it, consolidate with RL: +9.8 pp pass@256 over the base.
- Dragoi et al., *Beyond Pass@k: Cover@τ*, [arXiv 2510.08325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08325): count problems solved with ≥ τ of completions; rankings change. (Our avg solve rate and "≥ half the time" rule are already Cover@0.5.)
- Ma et al., *Learning What RL Can't* (ReLIFT), [ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07527): interleave SFT on the hardest questions. Dan's ignition study is the same move in our setting.

## 3. Ranked hypotheses

Each entry: the hypothesis, its source, the test on our checkpoints, cost, what is already known that bears on it, the pre-registered expectation, and why it sits where it does. "Dan's family" = best-cap12 base, EI r1–r16, GRPO default / pass@k r1–r8, replay control, 3 seeds. Tier A is worth doing first; tier C is listed so no one proposes it again without a reason.

### Tier A: new, cheap, decisive for the paper

**H1. RL needs orders of magnitude fewer trainable parameters than SFT to reach the same solve rate, because a binary reward carries ≤ 1 bit per episode.** *Source:* Morris et al. 2026, Schulman et al. 2025, Donoway et al. 2026. *Test:* mask all but d parameters (random coordinates and TinyLoRA-style random projections, d ∈ {1, 13, 100, 10³, 10⁴, all}) and run (a) Dan's `grpo_state.py` for two round-equivalents and (b) SFT on the same budget of RL-found proofs (an EI round) from the same base, under the same mask; read group B and C, the test split, held-out greedy. *Cost:* GRPO at 2,048 rollouts is ≈ 20 s per update on an A40, so a two-round masked arm is ≈ 1 GPU-hour; 6 d × 2 algorithms × 3 seeds ≈ 36 GPU-hours ≈ $18. SFT arms are minutes. **This is the one hypothesis here that needs small RL runs; it is a scope change for the workstream and needs Charles.** *Known:* nobody on any branch has limited parameter count; the 13-parameter result has no random-subset control (ours would be the first). *Expectation:* RL at d = 100 recovers ≥ 80 % of full GRPO's group-B gain; SFT at d = 100 recovers < 30 %; at d = all they agree. If RL and SFT need the same d, the bits-per-episode story is wrong for a 9.56M model and the paper should not lean on it. *Why first:* it is Charles's idea in its sharpest form, it separates the algorithms by information content rather than by outcome, and it is untouched in the repo and thin in the literature (one paper, one model family, no controls).

**H2. The gap RL closes is low-dimensional and the dimension shrinks with RL; the dimension of what remains is what the crossover measures.** *Source:* Li et al. 2018 / Aghajanyan et al. 2020 (intrinsic dimension), Donoway et al. 2026 (bits per parameter 0.01–0.1 for elicitation, > 1 for teaching). *Test:* plan M1: random-subset SFT of the base on RL's found proofs, transfer read on a disjoint half, d₉₀ per θ_0 ∈ {base, r2, r4, r8}. CPU for d ≤ 10⁴ masks if steps are few; GPU otherwise. *Cost:* ≈ 7 GPU-hours or two laptop days. *Known:* Leon's EDL says RL "starts closer" on the small model; d₉₀ is the parameter-space version of the same question and is not confounded by starting loss the way EDL is (the read-out is solve rate on held-out problems, not codelength). *Expectation:* d₉₀(base → r8 gap on group B) ≤ 10⁴; d₉₀ falls ≥ 10× from θ_0 = base to θ_0 = r4; the group-C part of the gap has no d₉₀ below "all". *Why second:* it is the direct measurement behind Charles's first idea and the cleanest number to put on an x-axis against RL rounds.

**H3. RL's weight update is sparse, nearly full rank within the touched coordinates, off the base's principal directions, and smaller in KL than an SFT update that reaches the same solve rate; EI (SFT on own samples) sits between GRPO and the replay control.** *Source:* Mukherjee et al. 2025, Zhu et al. 2025, Shenfeld et al. 2026. *Test:* plan M5 on every checkpoint pair Dan already has (zero training): per-matrix ‖Δ‖, fraction of |Δ| > τ, participation-ratio rank, projection on the base's top-32 singular directions, KL(base ‖ θ) and KL(θ ‖ base) on held-out states; compare GRPO default / pass@k, EI, replay control, and the r9–r16 continuation. *Cost:* laptop, hours. *Known:* explicitly not computed on Dan's side (`kl-update-size` dropped). *Expectation:* GRPO deltas ≥ 2× sparser at τ = 10⁻⁴ than EI's at equal textbook72; replay control the densest and most on-principal; KL(base ‖ GRPO r8) < KL(base ‖ EI r8) despite equal solve rates. If EI and GRPO look the same in weight space, "RL's Razor" does not distinguish them here and H1's prediction is in doubt before it is run. *Why third:* the cheapest new evidence in the whole list and a prerequisite for interpreting H1 and H2.

**H4. One correct proof per family is enough to unlock a family the base already holds (elicitation signature), and is not enough for the premise-count wall (teaching signature); that split predicts which pretrain-data pools will matter.** *Source:* Donoway et al. ICML 2026 ("a few bits in a single example"), Greenblatt et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2025 (one-example RL). *Test:* for each cell pretrain-data targets (4-premise, box-free ∧-shuffle, depth-3, 13+ lines, hypothetical syllogism, ∨E, DN), fine-tune the base and r8 on n ∈ {1, 4, 16, 64} proofs from the seed split (plus permutations) with the fixed algorithm and read the test-split cell; per-block codelength gives the EDL signature per family with starting loss reported next to it (Leon's confound) . *Cost:* 7 cells × 4 n × 3 seeds × 2 θ_0 = 168 short fine-tunes; CPU-feasible at n ≤ 16, ≈ 4 GPU-hours otherwise. *Known:* Dan did this for one family (excluded middle: teachable, not latent); the matched-distractor result (0 of 71 at ≥ 4 premises, every model) predicts the premise cell is teaching. *Expectation:* ∨E and DN cells move at n = 1–4 on the base (elicitation); 4-premise and 13+ lines do not move at n = 64 (teaching); r8 needs fewer examples than the base on every elicitable cell and no fewer on the teaching cells. *Why fourth:* it answers a question `pretrain-data` is about to spend GPU on (which pools), on CPU, before the pools are trained.

### Tier B: new or thinly covered, cheap, informative but not decisive alone

**H5. Extrapolating the RL weight delta (θ_base + α Δ, α > 1) raises large-k coverage above the RL model's own, which means RL stopped at a KL-anchored point short of what its own direction supports (stage-1 exploitation).** *Source:* Leon's interpolation result (α 1.25: pass@128 49.3 % vs 24.9 %), Shenfeld et al. 2026, Yao et al. 2025. *Test:* α ∈ {0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5} on Dan's family per round and arm; pass@k to 256 on groups B / C and the test split; also α applied to the r8 → r16 delta. *Cost:* sampling only, ≈ 1 GPU-hour or a laptop day. *Known:* one 3.2M model, one seed, no sealed-set gain at α 1.25 (3.4 % vs 19 % pass@1). *Expectation:* α 1.25 raises group-C pass@256 by ≥ 0.05 for EI with a pass@1 loss; if it also raises textbook test-split any-of-256, the "created" capability was already encoded in the delta direction and the crossover is a matter of step size, not of information. A null kills a cheap trick and supports the information account.

**H6. The per-round histogram of base-support classes of newly solved problems shifts from within-budget to created at a definite round, and that round is earlier for GRPO pass@k than for EI and never reached by the replay control.** *Source:* Yao et al. 2025 (two-stage view), Yuan et al. 2026, Dan's `rl-continue`. *Test:* plan M4 restricted to what is new: the class histogram per round r1–r16 and per arm from the found-proof files and the checkpoints; zero training. *Cost:* laptop, hours. *Known:* the base log p measurement itself is done three times; the time course by arm is not. *Expectation:* EI's new solves stay ≥ 70 % within-budget or latent through r8 and acquire a created tail only in r9–r16 (matching group C's rise); GRPO pass@k has the created tail by r4. This is the plan's crossover statement (H1–H3 in `plan.md` § 3) with the measurement cost removed.

**H7. EI's stall on group C through r8 is overtraining on saturated problems, not a capacity limit: restricting the fine-tune mix to problems with no success yet, or down-weighting solved ones, raises group-C pass@256 within 8 rounds.** *Source:* Yuan et al. 2026 (BBG), Dan's `frontier-supply` (+7.5 of 291, inside the MDD). *Test:* an EI ladder variant with `--alloc difficulty` (exists) or a found-proof weight that decays with a problem's running pass rate; 3 seeds. *Cost:* 3 ladders ≈ 9 GPU-hours ≈ $5; an RL run, so needs Charles. *Known:* Dan's rarity-filtered target supply was inside the noise; `--alloc` options exist and were not reported on group C. *Expectation:* group C pass@256 ≥ 0.15 by r8 (EI default 0.05); textbook72 unchanged. Ranked below H5–H6 because it is a method, and the paper is about measurement.

**H8. The EDL difference between base and RL survives controlling for starting loss only on streams the base has never seen (13+-line, 4-premise proofs), not on streams inside its support.** *Source:* Leon's finding that EDL tracks starting loss (Spearman 0.94); Donoway et al. Prop. 4.9 (fix the algorithm) and § 5.5 (format transients). *Test:* plan M2 with two changes: report EDL per nat of initial excess loss and the per-block curve shape, and pair every stream with a starting-loss-matched control stream. *Cost:* ≈ 20 GPU-hours as budgeted. *Known:* Leon's `edl_dan12.yaml` targets the same models; ask whether it ran before spending. *Expectation:* on D_RL(8) group-B proofs, base and r8 EDL per nat agree within 20 % (elicitation: RL only started closer); on D_gen16 the base's per-block curve rises then falls and r8's does not improve on it (neither has the capability). Ranked here because without the control it would reproduce Leon's confounded result on a bigger model.

**H9. The base's large-k curve on textbook72 and B ∪ C crosses the RL model's by k ≈ 4,096, as Yue et al. found, but only on the within-budget class; the created class never crosses.** *Source:* Yue et al. 2025, lit-review-2 recommendation (1), evidence-atlas gap. *Test:* a frozen best-cap12 read at k 4,096 (T 0.8 and 1.0) on textbook72 (aggregate) and the 322, with "covered" = log₂ p ≥ −log₂ k as the power-sampling null. *Cost:* 4,096 × 394 theorems ≈ 1.6 M attempts ≈ 2 GPU-hours per seed. *Known:* Leon's 3.2M curve crosses in 40 % of bootstraps; Dan's reads stop at 256 (1.2 M attempts only for 9 certified pairs). *Expectation:* no crossover on textbook72 in aggregate at k 4,096 (RL any-of-k stays ≥ base + 10); crossover on group B. Useful mainly because reviewers will ask for it.

### Tier C: already answered, or low information per dollar

**H10. Spurious-reward RL reproduces part of the textbook gain.** *Source:* Shao et al. 2025. *Why low:* the clipping bias that drives it needs a PPO-style clip; Dan's `grpo_state.py` is a plain policy gradient, so random rewards give a zero-mean gradient. `pretrain-data` E14 already tests the data-side version. Run only as a control if H1's RL arms are launched (one extra arm, $1).

**H11. Activation steering or reference grafting recovers the gap without weight updates.** *Source:* Venhoff et al. 2025, Le et al. 2026. *Why low:* Leon's additive steering is null on the small model; reference grafting works for *locked* capabilities (a thresholded gate), which is not our situation. Revisit only if H2 finds d₉₀ ≤ 100, where a single direction becomes plausible.

**H12. Cover@τ or a breadth-depth metric changes the EI vs GRPO ranking.** *Source:* Dragoi et al. 2025. *Why low:* the project's avg solve rate and "solved ≥ half the time" rule already are Cover@0.5 and the plan reports pass@k area with its decomposition; add τ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.9} to M3 as columns, no experiment.

**H13. Boundary-located teacher guidance (curriculum RL beyond the base) or interleaved SFT on the hardest problems expands group C.** *Source:* Cai et al. 2026, Ma et al. 2026 (ReLIFT). *Why low:* Dan's ignition study already shows one sibling proof injected at round 4 ignites dead arms (4 of 4) and `pretrain-data` is the workstream for supplying such data; nothing new to learn about measurement.

**H14. Averaging RL checkpoints across seeds or rounds expands coverage.** *Source:* model-soup literature; Dan's `ckpt-avg` (Stage-1 only, no). *Why low:* averaging removes exactly the rare modes a coverage measure wants; one CPU afternoon if H5 shows the delta direction matters, otherwise skip.
