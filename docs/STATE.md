[Round 2, run 4](../experiment-summaries/2026-09-18-round2-run4-grpo-vs-ei/README.md) (2026-09-18, reviewed, review linked in the summary): at an equal sample budget (256,000 samples, 256 per target) on the same six depth-3 f = 0 Stage-1 draws, on-policy GRPO (group-mean baseline, no KL, no retained data) reaches the depth-3 pattern in the first 62 updates on every draw, including the two rare-prior draws where expert iteration needs 2–4 rounds, and ends at 0.447–0.505 (G = 8) / 0.395–0.467 (G = 32) against expert iteration's 0.335–0.364, solving 11–34 % more targets; the acquired sets nest (the extra 89–144 theorems are the longer targets, oracle length 9–12) and every GRPO arm pays with held-out greedy 0.34–0.67 against 0.90–0.95. Every number reproduces; the reviewer rejects two readings: the six draws have non-zero base rates (5·10⁻⁶ – 1.9·10⁻³ per sample), so GRPO is a faster, less conservative eliciter of a rare-but-present prior and says nothing yet about ignition from a true zero, and the sprint's "GRPO saw nothing" may be a base-rate property of its draw rather than of its model, codec or targets. Next step: GRPO on the zero-base-rate draws (depth-3 a1 s5, reductio s1 / s2; G = 8, two sampling seeds, ≈ $1) and EI with an update every 512 samples on the same six draws to separate update frequency from the policy gradient.
