No knockout seed acquired any classical family by r16, and every full seed had at least one (Table~\ref{tab:primary},
Fig.~\ref{fig:endpoint}). This is the pre-registered primary indicator: 6/6 against 0/6, one-sided Boschloo
$p = 0.00024$. The same holds in the second sample seed, in the guided evaluation, and with the looser held-out sets
first pre-registered (178 theorems). Those were replaced by the strict sets in a dated amendment after round 1 of the
first knockout ladder, because that base proved 136 of 155 generated ``classical'' targets using a $\neg\neg$ that the
sequent supplied. The 0/6 bounds the per-seed rate at which this EI acquires a family from a \sig-free pretraining set
below 0.39 (one-sided 95\%).

The indicator is a weak statement about RL. Converse contraposition is acquired \emph{before} RL on 5/6 full bases (6/6
in the guided evaluation). Applied to the bases, the indicator already gives 5/6 against 0/6 ($p = 0.0032$; 6/6 against
0/6 guided, $p = 0.00024$; \emph{this paper}). Per family, the pre-registered rule gives \textsc{needs exposure} only
for converse contraposition (6 vs 0 seeds; Holm-corrected over six families, 0.0015). It gives \textsc{undetermined}
for negated conditional, Peirce and the Peirce sequent (3 vs 0 each; $p = 0.034$, Holm 0.17) and for excluded middle
(1 vs 0). It gives \textsc{neither} for De Morgan, whose four strict members no model proves. \rv{} The three
\textsc{undetermined} verdicts at $p = 0.034$ rest on the Holm correction over six families that the pre-registration
specifies for its secondary, per-family test; the decision rule itself reads one-sided Boschloo $p \leq 0.05$ per family,
and without the correction those three families would meet \textsc{needs exposure}. Holm is the conservative reading, and the review
applied it too.

The RL-phase comparisons were not pre-registered. The RL phase includes replay of pretraining data, so they compare the
whole RL phase, not RL proofs alone. During it, the full seeds gained 24, 69, 55, 42, 8 and 15 strict held-out theorems
and the knockout seeds 0, 2, 1, 0, 0 and 2. The arms do not overlap: exact one-sided Mann--Whitney $U = 36$, $p = 0.0011$
(\emph{this paper}). In the guided evaluations the gains are 5--62 against $-3$ to $+2$, again $p = 0.0011$. On the three
full seeds that ran on the knockout's hardware, $p = 0.012$. Five of six full seeds acquired a family during RL and no
knockout seed did ($p = 0.0032$). By coincidence this is the same 5/6 as the r0 indicator, from different seeds. On the
three new full seeds alone it is 2/3 against 0/6 ($p = 0.034$). On the training side, the knockout ladders proved none of
the 123 strict training theorems in round 1 and only 0, 5, 1, 0, 0 and 2 of them by round 16. The full ladders went
from 21, 1, 27, 17, 15 and 20 in round 1 to 49, 80, 86, 78, 33 and 36 (Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}a). No knockout ladder ever
had proofs for half of any family's strict training theorems (Appendix~\ref{app:shares}).

\subsection{One percent is enough}
\label{sec:dose}

The dose bases proved 6, 18 and 8 strict held-out theorems at r0, and 58, 39 and 30 at r16. All three acquired families
during RL; dose s1 had already acquired converse contraposition at r0. That is 3/3 against the knockout's 0/6 (Boschloo
$p = 0.0033$). This arm was added by a dated amendment before it ran, and it is exploratory. At r16 the dose seeds lie
inside the full arm's range (two-sided Mann--Whitney $p = 0.55$ with three seeds; \emph{this paper}). The dose restores
about a tenth of the full set's \sigp{} share (1.00\% against 10.94\%), and it moves the constituents little. In \KTdose{},
\texttt{DN} appears in 4.24\% of records and $\neg$-introduction in 6.12\%, against 3.29\% and 5.19\% in \KTko{} and
13.73\% and 15.21\% in \KT{}. Classical-only theorems are 1.56\% of a 20,000-record sample, against 1.33\% and 4.13\%
in \KTko{} and \KT{} by the same script (\emph{this paper} for \KTdose{}; the review's own sample of \KTko{} and \KT{} gives
1.35\% and 4.17\%). In round 1 the dose bases wrote 223, 183 and 241 \sig{} proofs, the new
full seeds 635--885 and the knockout seeds 6--13. The dose bases' rates were read only at $k = 256$ and in round 1, not
at large $k$. A second sample seed and a guided evaluation, run for this paper, prove 56, 39 and 30 (second seed) and
58, 42 and 29 (guided), with the same families (Appendix~\ref{sec:checks}).

\subsection{\sig{} is rare in the knockout arm, not absent}
\label{sec:rare}

\paragraph{The bases, sampled about a million times.} We drew 1.1--1.5 million attempts from each knockout base, spread
over the 106 strict held-out theorems (about $10^4$ per theorem, with sampling on a theorem paused after its first
success in each block of 1,024). Five of the six bases proved strict theorems: seed 1 seven times, seed 4 three times,
seed 5 twice, and seeds 2 and 3 once each. Every success is a genuine \sig{} proof. The successes are concentrated
(Table~\ref{tab:fambase}). Eleven are on converse contraposition, the family the full bases mostly had before RL, and
two are on the generated theorem \texttt{la\_transfer\_916}. On the other five families the six bases together wrote one
\sig{} proof in 6.7 million attempts. Excluded middle had none in 2.9 million. Pooled rates per base are 0 to
$6\times10^{-6}$ per attempt. On the theorems with a success, the per-theorem estimates are $7.5\times10^{-5}$ to
$4.9\times10^{-4}$, each from one to three successes in an early-stopped sample, which biases them upward. A fresh
sample of seed 1's members (\S\ref{sec:ko-s1}) found three of its four such members at 0 in 18,432 attempts. With guided decoding, at about 2.6 times the cost per attempt,
four of the six bases proved a strict theorem in 256 attempts at r0: seeds 0, 1, 3 and 4.

\paragraph{The ladders.} Every knockout ladder wrote \sig{} in round 1, 6--13 distinct proofs per ladder, on theorems
that supply $\neg\neg G$ or whose goal is a negation. Over 16 rounds each wrote about 2,200--4,400 distinct accepted
proofs using \sigp{} (0.33--0.64\% of its distinct proofs; \emph{this paper}, from the recount's counts and shares),
nearly all of that kind. Its fine-tuning mixes carried 16--276 \sig{} records per round (each proof repeated four times;
844--3,272 records over rounds 1--16). The free positive reductio was rare. Over 16 rounds the knockout ladders produced
0, 34, 4, 1, 2 and 16 distinct proofs using it, first in rounds never, 9, 12, 15, 15 and 2. The full ladders produced
17,507--50,222 each, and the dose ladders 20,597--26,662, from round 1 (Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}b). EI practised \sig{}
where the problem supplied the double negation, and rarely carried it to where it did not.

\paragraph{Beyond the waiting-time null (\emph{this paper}, post hoc).} Suppose each knockout ladder kept sampling the
123 strict training theorems at its base's pooled rate, with held-out rates standing in for training ones. It would then
expect a first strict success only after dozens to hundreds of rounds: 47 for seed 1 and 115--340 for seeds 2, 4 and 5,
with plug-in rates on the 105 family members. The expected number of seeds with a strict success by round 16 is 0.55.
Three had one: seed 1 in round 9, seeds 2 and 5 in round 14. Under the null that has probability 0.006. Under other
reasonable choices the probability rises to \rv{} 0.020, and the expected number of seeds to 0.74. Those choices are the 106
pool, family weights matched to the training mix (converse contraposition is 22\% of the strict training theorems and 17\%
of the held-out ones), and a Jeffreys posterior on each rate. Either way, unless the strict training theorems were easier for the bases than the
held-out ones, the knockout ladders' rate rose during RL. Why it rose is not identified: practice on cued \sig{}, general drift from fine-tuning, or both. It did not rise enough to
acquire a family.

\paragraph{Likelihood.} We scored the best known \sig{} proof of each of 101 strict held-out theorems under the r0, r4,
r8, r12 and r16 checkpoints of the twelve new ladders and under full seed 0's base, teacher-forced at $T=0.8$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:logp}). \rv{} The proofs are those the earlier full ladders (s0--s2) found,
not the scored ladders' own. At r0 the median
log-probability is $-15.4$ to $-20.9$ nats under the four full bases scored, $-18.9$ to $-25.3$ under the dose bases,
and $-26.0$ to $-36.0$ under the knockout bases. Full and dose overlap; the nearest dose and knockout bases are within
0.7 nats. The costliest step is the box that assumes $\neg G$: $-18.0$ to $-21.6$ nats under the knockout bases, $-10.5$
under full seed 0's base. Two ladders move strongly toward these proofs by r16 (full s3 $+10.2$ nats, dose s0 $+16.6$).
The other full and dose ladders move $+1.6$ to $+4.2$, and the knockout ladders $-7.5$ to $+3.7$. The path matters as
well. By r4, knockout seeds 2 and 3 had risen 8.4 and 8.0 nats, more than full seeds 4 and 5 ($+5.0$, $+4.1$) and dose
seeds 1 and 2 ($+1.6$, $+2.7$), and then they fell back. At every checkpoint the levels separate the knockout arm from the full and dose arms,
which overlap; the changes do not. Two caveats apply.
The scores condition on one of the 33 line-numbering offsets; subtracting $\ln 33 \approx 3.5$ nats gives a lower bound
on the proof's log-probability. And a knockout ladder that found its own spelling (seed 1, below) need not raise these.

\subsection{The one knockout ladder that moved}
\label{sec:ko-s1}

Knockout seed 1's free positive reductio kept spreading, so it was continued to round 24 (exploratory). Its free positive
reductio proofs per round rose from 11 in round 16 to 123 in round 24. From round 21 it had proofs for 9 of the 27
strict converse-contraposition training theorems, and it never got past 9 strict training theorems in all. By the
earlier organisms' criterion for igniting a pattern (at least 2\% of the targets that require it), it ignited at round 13
(3 of 123), but it did not acquire a family. At r16 it proved two strict held-out theorems in the main evaluation,
\texttt{la\_transfer\_916} and \texttt{1460}; both fresh r16 draws for this paper also found \texttt{1019} once. At r20
and r24 it proved the same five, each more often than at r16: four converse-contraposition
members and \texttt{916}. The largest gain is on \texttt{la\_transfer\_1019}, from 0 to 32 to 214 successes in 256
attempts at r16, r20 and r24 (the experiment's per-member counts, not recounted). Its r20 proofs of these
theorems take three routes: \sig{} (32 proofs), other \sigp{} (24) and the lemma route (7), a route absent from every
pretraining set.

Is this elicitation of something the base already did? The review read it so. In the large-$k$ sample the base had
proved four of its 18 converse-contraposition members, one to three times each in 7,168--10,240 early-stopped attempts,
and three of them (\texttt{1246}, \texttt{1460}, \texttt{2256}) are among the four the ladder proves. A pre-registered
re-sample for this paper, 18,432 attempts per member with no early stopping (C4, Appendix~\ref{sec:checks}), does not
support that reading. By the pre-registered criterion ($\hat p \geq 1/K$, here at least two successes) the base reaches
four of these 19 theorems: \texttt{916} (13 successes), \texttt{1460} (4), and \texttt{1096} and \texttt{1606} (2 each).
The ladder proves the first two, the two its base proved most often, in every r16 draw, and never proves the other two.
The three members it proves often from r20 had no success in the re-sample: \texttt{1019}, the largest gain, which \rv{} two
of the four plain r16 draws had already proved once (the guided draw, twice), and \texttt{1246} and \texttt{2256}, which had one success each in the
earlier sample. We had registered that if at most one of \texttt{1246}, \texttt{1460} and
\texttt{2256} was within reach, the paper would drop the reading that the ladder amplified mostly members its base could
already reach. Only \texttt{1460} is, so we drop it. Pooled over the 18 members, the re-sample's rate (10 successes in
331,776 attempts, $3.0\times10^{-5}$) matches the earlier sample's (6 in 186,172, $3.2\times10^{-5}$). What does not
replicate is which members succeed: at rates of $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$, about $10^4$ attempts per member cannot rank
the members. The other knockout seeds say little at the member level, because their ladders prove members only once or twice in
256 attempts. Seed 4's base proved three converse-contraposition members once each in early-stopped samples, and its
ladder proves no strict theorem at r16. Seed 5's base proved \texttt{1811}; its ladder proves \texttt{1246} and
\texttt{2256}, the members seed 1's ladder proves from r20, \rv{} in four and three of the four plain r16 draws respectively, and
\texttt{1811} in none. Seed
2's base proved \texttt{1096}; its ladder proved \texttt{1963} in the main evaluation and \texttt{1096} in both fresh
draws (one of which also found \texttt{1963}).
At these counts, single successes, of a base or of a ladder, do not settle which members EI amplifies.

\subsection{What else the knockout changes}
\label{sec:side}

\paragraph{Data.} Removing \sigp{} records also thins its constituents. In \KTko{}, \texttt{DN} appears in 3.29\% of
records (13.73\% in \KT{}) and $\neg$-introduction in 5.19\% (15.21\%). Theorems with no intuitionistic proof fall from
4.17\% to 1.35\% of a 20,000-record sample; the rest use \texttt{DN} on a $\neg\neg$ that a premise or an assumption
supplies. Mean proof term size moves from 35.91 to 36.25 nodes. The knockout removes the composition, and it also makes
each constituent three to four times rarer.

\paragraph{General ability.} The knockout bases are 6.88 points worse at greedy decoding of 5,000 held-out generator
theorems (0.8829 vs 0.9517). The gap comes from the 679 theorems whose reference proof uses \sig{} (0.45 vs 0.96); on
the other 4,321 the knockout is 0.12 points better. With 256 attempts, though, the knockout bases also prove 4.3 fewer
of the 225 intuitionistic theorems of holdout250, and 4.0 fewer of textbook72's 53 (three full seeds against six
knockout seeds). By r16 the holdout250 gap closes (0.996 vs 0.996), while textbook72 keeps $-2.5$ \rv{} on its 53 intuitionistic theorems ($-7.5$ on all 72).

\paragraph{Gains elsewhere.} On the 322 evaluation theorems, the base was given 512 attempts and then at least
$\Ktrain(16)$ on every theorem it failed. Within that budget the knockout bases reach 241--259 theorems and the full bases s3--s5
255--261; at r16 the knockout models prove 277--286 and the full models 291--298. RL's gain over what its own base reaches
(\dcov{}) is $+37$, $+36$ and $+32$ for full seeds s3--s5 and $+21$ to $+37$ for the knockout seeds. The means are 35.0
and 29.8, one-sided Mann--Whitney \rv{} $p = 0.095$ (exact, allowing for the one tie; \emph{this paper}). Part of the knockout's gain is
catching up on its own pretraining deficit. The knockout ladders may be somewhat worse at RL in general. They are not
clearly so, and the difference is small beside 8--69 against 0--2 strict theorems. These sets are not netted against
RL-free controls.

\paragraph{What this means for the interpretation.} Two things favour exposure to the composition over a general
deficit. The general costs are a few theorems out of hundreds. And the dose arm restores acquisition while moving
\texttt{DN} and $\neg$-introduction by about one point each (\S\ref{sec:dose}). The design cannot separate exposure in
pretraining from exposure through replay during RL (\S\ref{sec:setup}). Nor can it separate exposure to the composition
from the constituents' frequency, which a matched-frequency knockout would \citep{abdulsalam2026rewrite,keysers2020cfq}.

\subsection{The base's prior predicts what is proved at r16 (exploratory)}
\label{sec:prior}

Across the 13 ladders with a scored base, the base's median log-probability of known \sig{} proofs is rank-correlated
with strict held-out theorems proved at r16 (Spearman $\rho = 0.88$; Fig.~\ref{fig:prior}). This is mostly the
difference between arms: $\rho = 0.39$ ($p = 0.38$) without the knockout. Within a ladder, the base's log-probability of
a member's best known proof predicts whether the member is proved at r16. The AUC is 0.92--0.95 on the four full seeds
with scored bases (pre-registered expectation $\geq 0.7$) and 0.85--0.86 on the three dose seeds. Restricted to members
not proved at r0, so that only RL-phase gains count (post hoc, Addendum 5), it is 0.83--0.93 (full) and 0.74--0.84
(dose). Comparing only members
of the same family, the AUC is 0.47--0.98 (full) and 0.28--0.60 (dose). \rv{} One full seed (s3) is higher within families than
overall, and one (s5) is below 0.5 (0.47), against the expectation stated before computing. Each part of that
expectation (lower than overall; above 0.5) holds on three of the four full seeds, the minimum it set. The base's log-probability also predicts \emph{when} a member
is first proved in the ladder's per-round samples. The Spearman $\rho$ with the first-solve round is $-0.69$ to $-0.87$ on
the full seeds and $-0.61$ to $-0.72$ on the dose seeds. Within families it is $-0.33$ to $-0.48$ (full) and $-0.22$ to
$+0.14$ (dose). On the dose seeds, then, the prior predicts across families and not within them (all \emph{this paper}).
Reference-solution likelihood predicts success at larger $k$ \citep{schaeffer2025generative}, and RL gains concentrate
where the base already succeeds sometimes \citep{zhang2025interplay}.

\subsection{Two nulls}
\label{sec:nulls}

\paragraph{One seeded round.} From five knockout r16 checkpoints we ran one more EI round, with and without four copies of
one free positive reductio proof written by knockout seed 1's base (of \texttt{la\_transfer\_916}). Seeded minus control
was $+0$, $+0$, $+2$, $+0$ and $+0$ strict family members on seeds 0, 1, 2, 3 and 5 (seed 4 was not run). Seed 2's two
were single successes in 256 attempts (one-sided exact $p = 0.25$). This contrasts with the earlier, smaller organism
\rv{} (the 3.2M-parameter whole-proof models of round 2 run 3, judged by the project's older checker, not by Lean alone),
where a single injected sibling proof put the reductio on 6 and 17 targets in the next round
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}). Two explanations remain open: this organism resists one-proof seeding, or the read-outs
differ. The injected proof here is of a non-family theorem, and the outcome is strict held-out members rather than
training targets.

\paragraph{Power sampling.} Metropolis--Hastings sampling from $p^\alpha$ of the full and knockout bases
\citep{karan2025power} ($\alpha = 2$, 4; 32 chains per theorem) proved none of the theorems RL r8 proves beyond the base's
reach (0/33, 0/27), and none of the 106 strict theorems for knockout bases s0 and s1. Plain sampling with the same decoded
tokens proved 0--2. The sampler also failed its positive control: on 17 theorems the base proves rarely, it proved 0/17,
where plain sampling at the same tokens proved 6/17 ($T=0.8$) and 13/17 ($T=1.0$). This is a null for this sampler at
these settings, not for sharpening. Practical power samplers do not converge to $p^\alpha$ at realistic budgets
\citep{talwar2026power}. Details are in Appendix~\ref{app:power}.

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:robust}

The independent re-check accepted every stored knockout proof of a strict theorem in Lean 4.34.1 (448/448), the version
used in the loop. It rejected all 150 re-submitted Lean rejections, all 60 proofs paired with the wrong theorem, all 40
strict knockout proofs with \texttt{Classical.byContradiction} removed, and \texttt{sorry}. \rv{} Every Lean-gate
rejection in the experiment was the pre-filter's, so its false-reject rate matters. Most failed attempts never reach the
gate: in the knockout r16 evaluations, 72--88\% of attempts (77--89\% with cut-offs) end earlier, in the environment's
grammar and action checks, and only 10--23\% reach it. Neither check below covers those earlier failures. Re-checking 150 rejections per reason for its 13
reasons gave 0 accepted, a 95\% bound of 2.0\% per reason. For this paper, every pre-filter rejection in knockout
seed 1's ladder (\rv{} 222,135 rejected rows in rounds 1--16: 210,151 distinct texts, 21,055 of them on a by-contradiction
step) was re-checked in Lean, and none was accepted. That bounds the pre-filter's false-reject rate in that ladder
at \rv{} $1.4\times10^{-5}$ per distinct text ($1.4\times10^{-4}$ on by-contradiction steps; one-sided 95\%; C5). The
same harness accepted 400 of 400 texts the run's Lean gate had accepted, and none of them when paired with another
theorem. \rv{} An independent checker written for this paper's review also accepted none of the 210,151 distinct texts.
The knockout ladders' counts (strict training theorems,
free positive reductio proofs, the waiting-time observations) rest on the pre-filter rejecting no valid proof, which C5
supports for seed 1's ladder only. It does not cover the large-$k$ samples behind the certificates, whose rejected texts
were not stored. Cut-offs (a step over 512 tokens or a proof over 96 steps) can only hide successes. On the strict theorems at r16
they affect 0.04--4.97\% of knockout attempts, 0.11--2.89\% of full and 0.68--8.84\% of dose attempts. \rv{} Same-seed
re-evaluations of ten r16 checkpoints with standard and doubled caps, run for this paper, differ by at most one strict
theorem per checkpoint and in no family verdict; they are independent draws, so these $\pm1$ differences are sampling,
not cap effects (C1, Appendix~\ref{sec:checks}). The training theorems share no renaming class with any evaluation set, and no
pretraining set shares one with the 106 strict theorems (the pretraining sets do share 21--22 classes under a
premise-order-invariant key with the 5,000 greedy-decoding theorems).
