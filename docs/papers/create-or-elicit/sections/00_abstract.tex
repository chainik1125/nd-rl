Whether reinforcement learning (RL) against a verifier creates capabilities or amplifies behaviour the
base already had is contested; large-model studies can rarely change what the base saw. We study it in a
9.56M-parameter transformer prover trained from scratch on 155,000 synthetic propositional-logic proofs and judged only
by Lean~4. We remove one inference composition from pretraining, the classical reductio \sig{} (assume $\neg G$, refute
it, eliminate the double negation), keep both of its rules (each 3--4 times rarer), refill the data to
the same size, and run 16 rounds of expert iteration (EI), a rejection-sampling form of RL. No knockout seed acquired a
classical theorem family (half its held-out members proved in 256 attempts): 0/6 against 6/6 full-exposure seeds, a
pre-registered contrast ($p=0.00024$) largely present before RL. During RL, full-exposure seeds gained 8--69 held-out
classical theorems and knockout seeds 0--2; 5/6 against 0/6 acquired a new family ($p=0.0032$; not pre-registered).
Restoring \sig{} in 1\% of pretraining restored acquisition (3/3 seeds; exploratory). The knockout bases were not
\sig-free: five of six proved held-out theorems with \sig{}, mostly in one family, 1--7 times in 1.1--1.5 million
attempts. EI practised \sig{} where the double negation was supplied but rarely carried it to theorems that require building one. In
three earlier full-exposure runs, 51--60 theorems per run look RL-created at equal sample counts; 14--21 remain when the
base gets RL's compute as attempts. In this organism EI amplified a composition pretraining had exposed, but within 16
rounds did not acquire the classical families without it.
