\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scale and domain.} One architecture with 9.56M parameters, propositional logic over at most four atoms,
and pretraining proofs of at most 12 lines. Nothing here is measured on a large language model or on first-order logic.
The organism was chosen because it allows interventions that large models do not, and its results are results about
it.
\item \textbf{Near-saturated pools.} Outside the strict theorems, the evaluation pools are close to their ceilings
after RL: \rv{} the r8 models of the earlier full seeds s0--s2 prove 236--240 of holdout250, and the r16 models 277--303
of the 322 (303: the earlier full seed s1, in its x1 evaluation). Gains, \dcov{} and the
test-time-compute comparisons on these pools are compressed by the ceiling. That is why the paper leans on textbook72
and on the strict theorems, which no model saturates (at most 80 of 106). \rv{} One textbook72 problem
(\texttt{textbook\_3ed45280}\ldots) has a premise-swapped twin in every pretraining set: \KT{}, \KTko{} and \KTdose{}
each share its premise-order-invariant renaming class (no order-sensitive one), and none shares a class with the strict
theorems.
\item \textbf{One RL algorithm, 16 rounds, one composition.} Expert iteration at $k=32$, $T=0.8$ is rejection-sampling
fine-tuning. GRPO-style updates, more exploration, demonstrations or longer runs could behave differently, and in the
closest controlled study GRPO did, and in our earlier organism GRPO acquired a depth-3 nesting habit more often than EI;
in this organism, from full-exposure bases, plain GRPO proved fewer theorems than EI, which alone replayed pretraining
data (\S\ref{sec:discussion}). One knockout ladder was still
changing at round 24. Only one composition was removed.
\item \textbf{What the headline test measures.} The pre-registered indicator is largely decided before RL. The RL-phase
contrasts (gains during the RL phase, families acquired during it) were not pre-registered, and the RL phase includes
replay. On the three full seeds that match the knockout's hardware, the gains test gives $p = 0.012$ and the family
contrast 2/3 vs 0/6 ($p = 0.034$). \rv{} The free positive reductio (Table~\ref{tab:concepts}), used for the ladder
dynamics, is also a post hoc measure: it was defined after round 3 of the experiment and was not pre-registered.
\item \textbf{Seeds and power.} Six seeds per main arm power the primary indicator but not most per-family tests (three
families 3 vs 0, Holm 0.17; excluded middle 1 vs 0). A 0/6 outcome bounds the per-seed rate of acquisition after
\sig-free pretraining below 0.39 only. The dose arm has three seeds and two doses (0\% and 1\%) cannot locate a threshold.
\item \textbf{The knockout is not surgical, and exposure includes replay.} It makes \texttt{DN} and $\neg$-introduction
three to four times rarer and removes two thirds of the classical-only theorems. \rv{} It costs general ability
(\S\ref{sec:side}): at r0 the knockout bases are 6.88 points worse at greedy decoding of 5,000 held-out generator
theorems, all of it on the 679 whose reference proof uses \sig{}, and with 256 attempts they prove 4.0 fewer of
textbook72's 53 intuitionistic theorems and 4.3 fewer of holdout250's 225. Exposure in pretraining cannot be separated from exposure through replay during RL.
\item \textbf{``Absent'' is not established.} Five of six knockout bases proved strict theorems with \sig{} at large
$k$, and four of six under guided decoding at $k = 256$. Only one is certified out of reach as pre-registered: under plain
sampling, on a pooled mean rate, at one allocation, with an 8\% margin. \rv{} That holds at $T = 0.8$ on the
pre-registered 106 strict theorems (two of six certify on the 105 family members); plain sampling at $T = 1$ proved
\texttt{la\_transfer\_916} on the bases of seeds 0 and 1, and the pre-registered expectation of at least five certified
bases missed. Spreading the same compute over 106 or 1,054
theorems would certify 0 or 5 of 6. The certificate assumes the pre-filter rejected no valid proof in the large-$k$
sample. That could not be checked there; \rv{} in one knockout ladder (seed 1, rounds 1--16), none of the 222,135 rows
the pre-filter rejected (210,151 distinct texts) is Lean-accepted, which bounds its false-reject rate at
$1.4\times10^{-5}$ per distinct text ($1.4\times10^{-4}$ on by-contradiction steps; one-sided 95\%). Attempts that end
earlier, in the environment's grammar and action checks (72--88\% of knockout r16 attempts), are outside this check. No family could be certified at the sampling used, so the \textsc{created} verdict
was impossible by design. The ``rare'' evidence comes almost entirely from one family (converse contraposition); on the
others the bases sit at the sampling floor.
\item \textbf{Within-reach labels.} Per-theorem base rates in the knockout arm rest on one to three successes in fewer
than $K$ attempts, from sampling that paused after a first success. A fresh sample at $n \geq K$ of one base's members
(C4) found three of its four such members at zero, and two others with no early-stopped success within reach, so
these labels are unreliable in both directions. The other bases' members were not
re-sampled.
\item \textbf{Estimators.} The known-proof estimate runs two- to three-fold low below $10^{-4}$ and was calibrated on
full-exposure bases. The likelihood scores use \rv{} the earlier full ladders' (s0--s2) known proofs, not each scored ladder's own,
and one line-numbering offset.
\item \textbf{Budgets.} $K$ depends on the allocation, on the number of evaluation theorems it is spread over, and on
one measured GPU-class ratio. The knockout certificates and \dcov{} were computed at one allocation. \rv{} $K$ also
rests on one timing read per base of its cost per attempt ($c_{\mathrm{base}}$), whose co-tenancy was not recorded; a
read slowed by a shared card lowers $K$, so seed 0's certificate, with its 8\% margin, depends on it.
\item \textbf{Settings.} Old and new full seeds differ in GPU class, batch, resumption and evaluation-phase sampling,
and are pooled. Many evaluations cut off more than 0.1\% of attempts in some stratum (up to 8.8\% of strict-theorem
attempts on one dose seed). Doubling the caps moved each strict count by at most one theorem (Appendix~\ref{sec:checks}), \rv{} but cut the
cut-off share only 1.0--2.2-fold on nine of ten checkpoints: nearly all cut-offs that remain are single actions longer
than 1,024 tokens, runaway generations rather than proofs that need more room. The standard- and doubled-cap reads are
independent draws, not a paired comparison, so their $\pm1$ differences are sampling, not cap effects. The power-sampling comparators kept no proof text, so
their few successes were not re-checked in Lean.
\item \textbf{Interface.} In an earlier 3.2M organism, a base acting through the proof-state interface found at
least one proof of 28 of the 29 theorems that whole-proof EI had ``created'' (in up to 400,000 attempts; at EI's own
bar of $\hat p \geq 0.01$, 21 and 14), with two seeds per arm and the state confounded with the whole step interface. Whether creation verdicts depend on the interface is untested at this scale.
\end{itemize}
