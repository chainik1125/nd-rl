\section{How much did RL create in the full-exposure models?}
\label{sec:case}

The knockout asks whether EI can acquire a composition the base was not shown. Most claims in the literature are
instead about a single RL run and its own base. Before the knockout, the project applied the protocol of
\S\ref{sec:setup} to the three earlier full-exposure seeds (s0--s2, r0 vs r8, on the 322 evaluation theorems). This
section summarises what it found. Every number is from reviewed reports of those runs or was recounted by the
create-or-elicit review, except the $\Kadapt$ values, the 9 pre-selected pairs' count at $\Kadapt$, \dcov{} at allocations other than
$\Kpub$, and the 75--78\% share, which were not recounted (Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}).

\paragraph{The budget.} The five allocations of \S\ref{sec:setup} give five values of $K$ (Table~\ref{tab:budgets}).
$\Kpub$ is the value an earlier report used: the ladder's RTX A6000 seconds divided by a per-attempt cost measured on
hard theorems on an A40. The base costs 6.36--8.04\,ms per attempt on these theorems on an A40, and we convert A6000 seconds at a
measured A6000/A40 ratio of 0.932. The compute-matched set is a curve in $K$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}). Over a 12--16-fold
range of $K$ it falls from 34/33/33 to 21/20/14 theorems for s0/s1/s2. These 21/20/14 are compute-matched under all five
allocations, and 18/17/8 of them had no base success in at least 66,304 attempts. At the level of sets, RL stays ahead
of its base: \dcov{} is $+21/+16/+12$ at $\Kpub$, and positive for every seed at the other four allocations (not
recounted). Unlike the large-$k$ crossovers
reported for LLMs \citep{yue2025doesrl}, the base here does not catch up at $10^4$--$5\times10^4$ attempts per theorem.

\paragraph{Brackets, certificates and controls.} Sampling resolves success rates only down to about $1/n$. Below that we
estimate them from likelihoods. The \emph{known-proof estimate} sums the base's probability, teacher-forced at $T=0.8$
over the 33 line-numbering offsets, of every Lean-accepted proof of the theorem that any model found. It recovers a
median 0.98/0.94/0.93 of the measured rate on theorems with 3--100 successes in 512 attempts. It runs about two-fold low
at $10^{-5}$--$10^{-4}$ (median ratio 0.45) and lower still below (0.36 on two theorems; Appendix~\ref{app:budgets}).
Because the ratios are binned by measured rate, they condition on at least one success. Of the 9 pre-selected pairs, 7
certify at $\Kpub$. Four of them, on three theorems, also had no success from the RL-free controls (512 attempts each)
or from any other seed's base, though other seeds' bases tried \texttt{la\_transfer\_205} only 17,408 and 66,560 times
and \texttt{1833} 17,408 times each. Only one of the three, a Peirce instance, is classical-only. The two controls
receive training without RL's proofs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{replay-only}: eight fine-tuning rounds on pretraining replay alone (on 1.3--1.6 times fewer tokens than
the ladder). It proves 239/244/249 of the 322,
against the base's 232/236/234 and r8's 286/286/293.
\item \emph{continued pretraining}: about 75--78\% of the ladder's GPU time. It proves 220/215/219, gaining 12--19
theorems and losing 30--42.
\end{itemize}
The cheaper control is informative: replay alone recovers 13--25\% of RL's gain on the 322.

\paragraph{Likelihoods along RL.} On theorems RL newly proves, the hardest step of r8's most likely proof rises from
about $-6.2$ nats at r0 to about $-1.3$ at r8, mostly by round 4 (teacher-forced at $T=1$). Part of this is by
construction, since the proof is selected at r8. RL also lifts the pretraining reference proofs of the same theorems,
about three times less. On theorems neither r0 nor r8 proves, the reference proofs do not improve.

\paragraph{Test-time compute before and after RL.} If RL mostly converts rare-but-possible proofs into likely
ones, extra compute at test time should help the base more than the RL model (Fig.~\ref{fig:ttc}). Unless stated
otherwise, the models here are the earlier full-exposure seeds s0--s2 at r0, r8 and r16. A guided evaluation
adds 18--22 holdout250 theorems to the base and $-1$ to $+4$ to the RL models. On holdout250 the RL models are within
3--14 theorems of the ceiling, and guided reading halves the gap between the base and r8 (44/31/37 theorems plain,
21/14/19 guided). Textbook72 is the fairer pool: there, guided redraws add 8--12 to the base and 3--6 at r8. Guided attempts cost about 2.2 times the GPU time of plain ones (measured on r8 models), so this is not
compute-matched. At matched
sampled tokens, guided redraws still beat plain resampling on long textbook problems for six r8 models (three of them pretrained
on proofs of at most six lines), by 5.2--10.3 points. A PUCT tree search over proof states, with a value head trained on the frozen policy's rollouts, was compared
with sampling at equal wall-clock. At r8 it is within $\pm3$ theorems of sampling on every pool, and it failed its
pre-registered gate on the theorems neither r0 nor r8 proves. At r0 it adds 10--19 theorems on 100 long theorems
(13--16 lines), about 44\% of what eight EI rounds add there by sampling. That fraction is 14\% on holdout250 and 17\% on
textbook72. Guided decoding does not recover most of the compute-matched creations: the guided base proves 3/4/1
of the 22/21/14 at $\Kpub$. DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5 reported its search and RL gains as orthogonal \citep{xin2024deepseekprover}. Here they overlap:
search recovers part of RL's gain at the base and adds little after RL.
