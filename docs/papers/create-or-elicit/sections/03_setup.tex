\section{The organism and the experiment}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Logic, data and judge.} Theorems are sequents $\Gamma \vdash G$ of propositional logic over at most four
atoms, with $\land,\lor,\to,\neg,\bot$. Proofs are Fitch-style natural deduction: premises, assumptions that open boxes,
the introduction and elimination rules of each connective, $\bot$-elimination and double-negation elimination
(\texttt{DN}). The pretraining set \KT{} (``K12'' in the released code) holds 155,000 synthetic proofs of 2--12 lines from a
generator, with a flat length distribution. A proof counts if and only if Lean~4 core accepts it. A syntactic pre-filter may reject
malformed text before Lean, but never accepts anything on its own (\S\ref{sec:robust}).

\paragraph{Model and interface.} Every model is the same decoder-only transformer: 6 layers, width 384, ALiBi and NoPE
attention heads, 9,560,832 parameters. It is pretrained from scratch for 1,200\,s on one NVIDIA A40, about 24,000 steps
or 3.7 passes over the (state, action) pairs of \KT{}. It acts in a \emph{proof-state environment}. It sees the focused
goal's Lean tactic state and writes one step, such as \texttt{have n5 : $\neg$P := n1 n4}. The environment applies the
step, opens and closes the boxes of $\to$- and $\neg$-introduction and the branches of $\lor$-elimination, and ends the
attempt at the first step it cannot apply. It also numbers the lines from one of 33 random starting offsets, so every
proof has 33 spellings. The finished proof is rendered in Lean and judged.

\paragraph{Expert iteration.} Each round, the current model samples $k=32$ attempts at temperature $T=0.8$ on each of
4,495 training theorems. Accepted proofs accumulate across rounds, up to four per theorem. The model is then fine-tuned
for 600 steps, from where it left off, on those proofs (each repeated four times) mixed with 20,000 records replayed
from its own pretraining set. The training budget is the same for every theorem. This is rejection-sampling fine-tuning
in the lineage of expert iteration \citep{anthony2017exit,polu2022curriculum}, STaR \citep{zelikman2022star} and
ReST$^{\mathrm{EM}}$ \citep{singh2023restem}. We call one 16-round run from one pretrained seed a \emph{ladder}, and
the model after $N$ rounds r$N$; r0 is the pretrained base. Each round also samples $k=32$ attempts on every held-out
family member, which is never trained on.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} An evaluation draws $k=256$ independent attempts per theorem at $T=0.8$, with at most 96 steps
and 512 tokens per step. A theorem is \emph{proved} if any attempt is accepted. Two sample seeds give two independent
draws of the same checkpoint. A \emph{guided} evaluation uses the same budget, but a step that a per-step logical
checker rejects is redrawn from the same state without replacement, up to 10 times per attempt. On plain reads of
six earlier r8 models of the same network (run \run{guided-tts}: the full-exposure seeds s0--s2, and three seeds
pretrained on proofs of at most six lines), the checker never
rejected a step Lean accepts in 314,541 tested atomic (state, step) pairs. General ability is measured on 5,000 held-out
generator theorems decoded greedily, and on 322 evaluation theorems (72 textbook problems plus 250 held-out generator
theorems; \S\ref{sec:case}).

\paragraph{The composition and the theorem families.} Table~\ref{tab:concepts} defines the terms used throughout
(glossary: Appendix~\ref{app:glossary}).
In \KT{}, \sig{} occurs in 16,703 records and \sigp{} in 16,961 (10.94\%); the lemma route occurs in none. We track six
classical generator families: converse contraposition ($\neg B \to \neg A \vdash A \to B$), negated conditional
(a negated implication entails its antecedent and the negation of its consequent), Peirce's law, the Peirce sequent ($(A\to B)\to A \vdash A$), excluded middle, and
De Morgan ($\neg(A\land B) \vdash \neg A \lor \neg B$). Each has 40 training members and 40 held-out members,
substitution instances of the family's schema. The strict members number 123 in training and 106 held out: 105 family
members plus one generated theorem, \texttt{la\_transfer\_916} (Table~\ref{tab:families}). A model \emph{acquires} a
family if it proves at least half of the family's strict held-out members in one evaluation. Figure~\ref{fig:lean}
shows a \sig{} proof written by a knockout base.

\paragraph{Arms.} The arms share the network, the pretraining recipe and the EI protocol (Table~\ref{tab:arms}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\Kko{}} pretrains on \KTko{}. This is \KT{} minus exactly its 16,961 \sigp{} records, refilled with
16,961 records drawn length by length from \KT{}'s own unused source pools. Every refill record passes \KT{}'s filters
and contains no \sigp{} and no lemma route. Under two renaming-invariant keys, the refill shares no theorem with \KT{}
or with any evaluation file. Lean accepts all 16,961 refill records (Appendix~\ref{app:k12ko}).
\item \textbf{\Kdose{}} restores 1,550 removed \sig{} records in place of refill records, at the length mix of \KT{}'s
\sig{} records: 1.00\% of the records.
\item \textbf{\Kfull{}} pretrains on \KT{}. Seeds s0--s2 come from earlier runs. They differ from the new seeds s3--s5
in three ways. Rounds 1--8 ran on RTX A6000 cards at sampling batch 2,048 (s2 at 1,024 from round 2), whereas the new seeds
ran on A40s at 1,024.
The ladders were resumed at rounds 9 and 13, which restarted the replay sampler. And each round's evaluation phase
sampled more theorems. Changing the batch changes which samples are drawn, not how they are judged.
\end{itemize}
Because each ladder replays its own pretraining data, exposure differs during RL as well. Full ladders replay about
2,200 \sigp{} records per round, dose ladders about 200, and knockout ladders none. ``Exposure'' below therefore means
pretraining together with the replay drawn from it. Arms with the same seed number share initial weights and sampling
seeds, not data.

\paragraph{Budgets, reach and certificates.} We write $p_M(t)$ for the probability that one attempt of model $M$ on
theorem $t$ is accepted, and $\hat p = c/n$ for its estimate from $c$ successes in $n$ attempts. For a budget of $K$
attempts per theorem:
\begin{align*}
t \text{ within reach of the base at } K \;&\Longleftrightarrow\; \hat p \geq 1/K, \\
t \text{ not reached at } K \;&\Longleftrightarrow\; c = 0 \text{ with } n \geq K, \\
t \text{ certified out of reach at } K \;&\Longleftrightarrow\; \mathrm{UB}_{95}(c, n) < 0.05/K,
\end{align*}
where $\mathrm{UB}_{95}$ is the one-sided 95\% Clopper--Pearson bound; with $c=0$, certification needs $n \approx 60K$.
``Within reach'' from a single success in fewer than $K$ attempts is weak evidence, and we flag it where it occurs. A
\emph{pooled} certificate over a set of theorems bounds their mean per-attempt rate, not any one of them. $K$ is
matched to RL's compute as $K = C_{\mathrm{RL}} / (N_{\mathrm{eval}}\, c_{\mathrm{base}})$. Here $C_{\mathrm{RL}}$ is
what the ladder spent, and $c_{\mathrm{base}}$ is the base's measured cost per attempt. ``What the ladder spent'' has several defensible
readings, each an \emph{allocation}: its sampling attempts on training theorems ($\Katt$); the GPU time of its sampling and
fine-tuning phases ($\Ktrain$; $\Ktrain(16)$ for a 16-round ladder); all of its GPU time ($\Kuni$); the value of an
earlier report ($\Kpub$, \S\ref{sec:case}); or an adaptive spend of 512 attempts on every theorem and the rest on the
base's failures ($\Kadapt$). $N_{\mathrm{eval}} = 322$ is the number of evaluation theorems the budget is spread
over. $N_{\mathrm{eval}}$ is a choice: spreading the same compute over the 1,054 theorems of our largest evaluation
would make every $K$ 3.3 times smaller. The \emph{compute-matched set} $\mathcal{C}(K)$ holds the theorems that RL
proves and its base cannot reach within $K$. $\dcov(K)$ is the number RL proves minus the number the base reaches.

\paragraph{The pre-registered decision rule.} Per family, at r16:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textsc{created} if at least 2 of 6 knockout seeds acquire it and the knockout base is certified out of reach on
it;
\item \textsc{needs exposure} if at most one knockout seed acquires it and more full seeds do (one-sided Boschloo test,
$p \leq 0.05$);
\item \textsc{neither} if no seed acquires it;
\item \textsc{undetermined} otherwise.
\end{itemize}
The primary test compares, per seed, the indicator ``at least one family acquired at r16'' between the full and
knockout arms. The pre-registration fixed the certificate pool as the 106 strict held-out theorems, at each knockout
seed's training-phase budget $\Ktrain(16)$. As sampled, about $10^4$ attempts per theorem, no single family could be
certified at that budget, so \textsc{created} could not be returned. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports what the
remaining verdicts can and cannot say.
