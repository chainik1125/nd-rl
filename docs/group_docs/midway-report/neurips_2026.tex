\begin{abstract}
  Behaviours learned after large amounts of Reinforcement Learning (RL) are increasingly the dominant source of safety concerns for the development and deployment of frontier models. The high RL regime is, however, expensive to access even for mid-size LLMs. To investigate the qualitative behaviours of pre-trained transformers in the high-RL regime, we therefore develop a synthetic setting based on formal logic. We show that we are able to access the high-RL regime in this setting, that qualitatively new capabilities emerge, and that these are associated with a breakdown of traiditonal measures of elicitation. Our results suggest that new safety techniques will be needed in the high-RL regimes, alongside model organisms to study them in an accessible way.
\end{abstract}
